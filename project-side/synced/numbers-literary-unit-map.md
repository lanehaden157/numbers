# Numbers — Literary Unit Map

Working map for the unit-by-unit study. There are 38 session-sized units, one four-pass walk each, grouped under nine movements for orientation. Hebrew terms appear in transliteration only.

**Names.** This map is exempt from the Moshe/Aharon/Yahweh convention: it is a reference file, so it keeps Moses, Aaron and the LORD. Spoken passes, handoffs and fragments use Moshe, Aharon and Yahweh. A built unit's title follows `data/units.json`, so U03 here differs from its fragment title only in that spelling.

## Ground rules this map follows

**Versification.** References are in **English** versification. Where the Hebrew (MT/OSHB) numbering differs, it appears in brackets. Numbers has two such stretches: Eng 16:36–17:13 = Heb 17:1–28, and Eng 29:40–30:16 = Heb 30:1–17. The candidate-boundaries list uses Hebrew numbering, so every marker cited in those stretches below has been converted.

**Markers.** "L" is the Leningrad Codex, from the candidate list (OSHB). "A" is the Aleppo Codex as Whitt reconstructs it. P is *petuhah* (open, a major break) and S is *setumah* (closed, a minor break). A marker is cited by the verse it follows, so "P after 1:54" means the break falls between 1:54 and 2:1. Most L/A disagreements are about the *type* of break, not whether a break exists. Two disagreements over whether a break exists fall on a unit boundary: 2:34/3:1 (A breaks, L does not) and 16:40/41 (A has a petuhah, L has none). Per Lane's call, neither codex governs. Both are noted, and narrative logic decides.

**Legal and ritual blocks** are handled case by case. Each entry for such a block says why it stands alone or joins a neighbour.

**The book's own macro-markers, weighted above imposed symmetry:**
- **The census bracket.** Ch. 1 counts the generation that dies. Ch. 26 counts the one that enters, and 26:64–65 points back to ch. 1 explicitly. This gives the two-part frame: 1:1–25:18 and 26:1–36:13.
- **Dated notices.** 1:1 (2nd month, 2nd year). 7:1 (the day the tabernacle was set up, i.e., 1st month; see Exod 40:17). 9:1 (1st month, 2nd year). 10:11 (20th of 2nd month, 2nd year). 20:1 (1st month, year unstated). 33:38 (Aaron's death, 5th month, 40th year). 7:1 and 9:1 are dated earlier than 1:1, so chs. 1–10 are not arranged in time order. This is a tension to hold, not a problem to fix.
- **The "plains of Moab" formula.** 22:1; 26:3, 63; 31:12; 33:48–50; 35:1; 36:13. It frames everything from Balaam to the colophon as happening in one place.
- **Departure and itinerary formulas** built on *nasa'* ("set out, pull up stakes"). They appear at 10:11–12, 10:33, 12:16, 20:22, 21:4, 21:10–20, 22:1, and through all of ch. 33.

### Digests (DRAFT)

Two to four sentences per unit, at most 80 words, written from the text. Shown on the peaks path for units that are not peaks.

- 01: Moses is told, on the first day of the second month of the second year, to count every man of fighting age by tribe and clan. The totals come to 603,550. The Levites are left out and appointed to guard the tabernacle, so that no wrath falls on the community. The book opens by numbering the generation that will not enter the land.
- 02: The twelve tribes camp in four groups of three, each under a standard, facing the tent of meeting from a distance. Judah leads on the east, then Reuben, Ephraim and Dan take the other sides. The Levites camp in the middle. The tent stands at the centre, and the march follows the same order. Israel does as the LORD commands.
- 03: The generations of Aaron and Moses open with the deaths of Nadab and Abihu. The Levites are given to Aaron and take the place of every firstborn Israelite. They are counted, 22,000 in all. The 273 firstborn beyond that number are redeemed at five shekels each, and the silver goes to Aaron and his sons.
- 04: The Kohathites, Gershonites and Merarites are assigned their loads, and Aaron and his sons must cover the holy things before the Kohathites carry them. The Kohathites must never see the holy things, even for a moment, or they die. The Levites aged thirty to fifty are counted: 8,580 for service.
- 05: The camp must be kept clean for the LORD who dwells in it, so those with skin disease or discharge are sent outside. A wrongdoer confesses and makes restitution with a fifth added. A husband who suspects his wife brings her to the priest, who administers the water of bitterness, and she is cleared or cursed.
- 06: A man or woman may take a Nazirite vow for a time. During it they abstain from wine, let the hair grow and avoid the dead, and at the end they offer sacrifices and shave the head. The LORD then tells Aaron how to bless Israel: bless you and keep you, make his face shine, lift up his face and give you peace.
- 07: On the day the tabernacle is set up, the twelve chieftains bring wagons and oxen, then on twelve days an identical offering each, listed in full. The total is tallied at the end. When Moses enters the tent to speak with the LORD, he hears the Voice speaking from above the cover between the cherubim.
- 08: Aaron sets up the seven lamps to light the front of the lampstand. The Levites are cleansed by water, shaving and a wave-offering, and presented to the LORD in place of the firstborn. Their service begins at twenty-five, and at fifty they retire from the heavy work but may still assist.
- 09: A second Passover is allowed to the unclean and travellers, one month later. The cloud covers the tabernacle by day and fire by night, and Israel breaks camp or stays by its movement, however long. Two silver trumpets are made to summon the community, signal the march and mark the festivals.
- 10: On the twentieth day of the second month the cloud lifts and Israel sets out in order, tribe by tribe. Moses asks Hobab to guide them; the ark goes ahead three days seeking a resting place. When it sets out Moses says, 'Arise, LORD, let your enemies be scattered', and when it rests, 'Return, LORD, to the countless thousands of Israel.'
- 11: The people complain and fire consumes the edge of the camp, at Taberah. Then they crave meat and weep for Egypt's fish. Moses protests that the load is too heavy. The LORD gives him seventy elders on whom the spirit rests, and sends quail until it comes out of their nostrils; a plague strikes the craving at Kibroth-hattaavah.
- 12: Miriam and Aaron speak against Moses over his Cushite wife and ask whether the LORD has not spoken through them too. The LORD answers from the cloud that he speaks to Moses face to face. Miriam is struck with skin disease; Moses prays 'Heal her, God, I beg you', and she is shut out seven days.
- 13: Twelve leaders spy out Canaan for forty days and return with grapes, praising the land. Ten say its people are too strong; Caleb says they can take it. The people weep, wish to die in Egypt, and talk of choosing a leader and going back. Joshua and Caleb plead, and the community threatens to stone them until the glory appears.
- 14: The LORD threatens to disown the people. Moses intercedes with the LORD's own words, slow to anger and abounding in covenant love. The LORD pardons but sentences: this generation dies in the wilderness, forty years, a year for each day of the spying. The ten spies die by plague. The people attack anyway without Moses or the ark and are routed at Hormah.
- 15: Just after the sentence, the LORD gives laws for offerings 'when you come into the land', and for unintentional and defiant sin. A man gathering sticks on the Sabbath is stoned. The unit ends with tassels on the garments, a reminder not to follow the heart and eyes, and to remember all the commandments.
- 16: Korah, Dathan and Abiram challenge Moses and Aaron, claiming the whole community is holy. Moses calls for a test of incense. The ground splits and swallows the rebels' households, and fire consumes the two hundred and fifty who offered incense.
- 17: The censers are hammered into plating for the altar as a reminder. The people blame Moses and Aaron, and a plague kills 14,700 until Aaron stands between the dead and the living with incense. Aaron's staff alone buds and bears almonds, and is kept before the ark as a sign. The people cry, 'We perish.'
- 18: The LORD tells Aaron that the priests and Levites bear the guilt of the sanctuary so that no outsider approaches it. The priests receive the offerings and firstfruits; the Levites receive the tithe. The priests have no territory, since the LORD is their portion and their inheritance, and the Levites give a tithe of their tithe.
- 19: An unblemished red cow is slaughtered outside the camp and burned, and its ashes are mixed into water of purification. Whoever touches a corpse, a bone or a grave is unclean seven days and is sprinkled on the third and seventh. The priest and the one burning it are themselves made unclean.
- 20: Miriam dies at Kadesh. With no water, the people quarrel with Moses and Aaron. The LORD tells Moses to speak to the rock, but he strikes it twice and water flows. For failing to trust and honour him before Israel, the LORD says that Moses and Aaron will not bring this community into the land. The place is called Meribah.
- 21: Edom refuses passage and Israel turns away. At Mount Hor Aaron dies and Eleazar is clothed in his garments; Israel mourns thirty days. The king of Arad attacks and takes captives. Israel vows to devote his cities to destruction, the LORD gives them over, and the place is named Hormah.
- 22: On the road around Edom the people complain again of the food. The LORD sends fiery serpents, and many die. The people confess, and at the LORD's command Moses makes a bronze serpent on a pole; whoever looks at it lives. The journey continues by stages, with the Book of the Wars and the song of the well.
- 23: Israel asks Sihon of the Amorites for passage; he refuses and fights at Jahaz, and is defeated. Israel takes his cities, including Heshbon, quotes a taunt-song over it, and then defeats Og king of Bashan. The unit ends with Israel camped on the plains of Moab across the Jordan from Jericho.
- 24: Balak king of Moab, afraid of Israel, sends elders to hire Balaam to curse them. God tells Balaam not to go or curse, and he refuses. A second, grander embassy comes; Balaam says he can only do what the LORD says. God permits him to go if the men call him, and Balaam rises and goes with them.
- 25: The LORD's angel stands in the road with a drawn sword, and the donkey sees him when Balaam does not. She turns aside, presses against a wall, and lies down, and Balaam beats her three times. The LORD opens her mouth, then Balaam's eyes. The angel tells him to go on, but to speak only what he is told.
- 26: At Balak's seven altars Balaam is given a word and blesses instead of cursing: 'How can I curse whom God has not cursed?' Balak moves him to a second place. The second oracle says God is not a man that he should lie; he has blessed, and Balaam cannot reverse it.
- 27: Balaam stops looking for omens, and the spirit of God comes on him. He blesses Israel: how fair your tents, a king higher than Agag. Balak, angry, dismisses him. Balaam's last oracles name a star out of Jacob and a sceptre out of Israel that will crush Moab, and then speak against Amalek, the Kenites and others.
- 28: Israel joins Moabite women in feasting and in the worship of Baal of Peor, and a plague begins. The LORD orders the leaders impaled. Phinehas son of Eleazar spears an Israelite and a Midianite woman together, and the plague stops with 24,000 dead. The LORD gives him a covenant of peace and a lasting priesthood.
- 29: After the plague the LORD orders a second census, by tribe, for dividing the land. The total is 601,730, and the Levites are counted apart. Of those counted at Sinai, none remain except Caleb and Joshua: the sentence of 14:29–35 is complete.
- 30: The five daughters of Zelophehad ask for their father's inheritance and Moses brings the case to the LORD, who rules they are right. Daughters will inherit when there is no son. Then Moses is told to see the land and die. He asks for a shepherd for the people, and Joshua is commissioned before Eleazar and the community.
- 31: The LORD lists the public offerings by time: the daily lamb at morning and evening, the Sabbath, the new moon, and Passover and Unleavened Bread. The long second half sets out the seventh month: trumpets, the Day of Atonement, and the eight days of Tabernacles, with an ever-shrinking number of bulls.
- 32: A man who makes a vow must keep it. A father may annul his daughter's vow, and a husband his wife's, on the day he hears of it; if he is silent, the vow stands. A widow or divorced woman must keep her own vows.
- 33: Moses sends a thousand from each tribe with Phinehas to avenge Israel on Midian. They kill the kings and Balaam and take the women, and Moses is angry that they were kept alive, since they caused the Peor trouble. The soldiers and priests are purified, the spoil is divided, and the officers give gold as a memorial.
- 34: Reuben and Gad, with large herds, ask to settle east of the Jordan. Moses asks whether their brothers should go to war while they sit here, and recalls the spies. They promise to cross armed and lead the fight, and Moses agrees on those terms. Half of Manasseh joins them.
- 35: Moses writes the stages of the journey from Egypt to the plains of Moab, forty-two stops, at the LORD's command. It begins at Rameses at the Passover and runs through Sinai, Kadesh, the death of Aaron on Mount Hor at 123, and the final stages east of the Jordan.
- 36: Crossing the Jordan, Israel must drive out the inhabitants and destroy their idols, or those left will be thorns in their eyes. The land is allotted by lot. The boundaries of Canaan are set out on the south, west, north and east, and a leader from each tribe is named, with Eleazar and Joshua, to divide it.
- 37: Forty-eight cities are given to the Levites, six of them cities of refuge. A killer by accident may flee there until the death of the high priest, but a murderer is put to death, on the evidence of witnesses. Blood pollutes the land, and the land must not be defiled, since the LORD dwells in it.
- 38: Heads of the Gilead clans fear that if the daughters of Zelophehad marry outside their tribe, the land will pass away. Moses rules that they may marry whom they like but only within their own tribe's clans. The daughters do as commanded, and the book ends with the colophon: these are the commands given on the plains of Moab.

## Unit entries

### PART ONE — 1:1–25:18

#### I. At Sinai: ordering the camp (1:1–10:10)

**U01 · 1:1–54 · The first accounting**
- *Markers:* L has a petuhah after each tribal count (1:19 through 1:43), then after 1:47 (setting apart the Levites) and after 1:54. A treats the first of these (after 1:19) as a setumah.
- *Grid:* Aligns with the chapter.
- *Commentators:* Levine takes 1–4 as one Part, "Getting Organized." Constable likewise groups chs. 1–4.
- *Why here:* The dated opening (1:1) and the closing compliance formula (1:54) frame the unit, and the Levite exemption (1:47–54) sets up U03–U04. The keyword *paqad* ("muster, count, attend to") opens here and is a thread for the whole book. The census numbers are one of the two places where Rashi and Ibn Ezra get real weight.

**U02 · 2:1–34 · The camp around the tent**
- *Markers:* Setumot divide the four standard-camps (2:9, 2:16, 2:17, 2:24), with a petuhah after 2:31. **L has no break at 2:34/3:1; A has a petuhah there.**
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Why here:* The tent sits at the centre and the tribes camp around it. This is the presence theme drawn as a picture.
- *Decision:* The boundary follows A and the chapter grid, because 3:1 opens with its own formula (next unit).

**U03 · 3:1–51 · Aaron's line and the Levites in place of the firstborn**
- *Markers:* P after 3:4 (Nadab and Abihu), P after 3:10, S after 3:13, S after 3:39, P after 3:43, P after 3:51.
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Why here:* Opens with *'elleh toledot* ("these are the generations of…") at 3:1. That formula is Genesis's own structuring device, used here for Aaron and Moses, so it is a strong book-internal marker. The unit runs through the firstborn-redemption arithmetic (3:40–51).

**U04 · 4:1–49 · Carrying the holy things**
- *Markers:* S after 4:16, P after 4:20, P after 4:28, S after 4:37, P after 4:49.
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Why here:* This is the transport manual for Kohath, Gershon, and Merari, closing with the census of Levites fit for service. 4:17–20 (the Kohathites must not see the holy things "even for a moment") is the crux.

**U05 · 5:1–31 · Keeping the camp clean: three laws**
- *Markers:* P after 5:4, P after 5:10, P after 5:31. These are three petuhah paragraphs: expelling the impure, restitution, and the suspected wife (*sotah*).
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Commentators:* Levine, "Aspects of Purity"; his ch. 5 is its own Part.
- *Legal handling — stands alone.* All three laws apply the camp-holiness logic of chs. 1–4 ("the camp where I dwell among them," 5:3). They are not interludes in a narrative, because at this point there is no narrative yet. The presence thread continues here.

**U06 · 6:1–27 · The Nazirite and the priestly blessing**
- *Markers:* P after 6:21. The blessing (6:22–27) has a setumah after every line (6:23, 24, 25, 26). Whitt notes this as the setumah's list-separating use. L has a petuhah after 6:27; A has only a setumah, so in A the blessing runs straight into 7:1.
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Commentators:* Levine takes ch. 6 as one Part.
- *Legal handling — stands alone.* The unit moves from lay holiness (the Nazirite vow) to the priests putting the Name on Israel (6:27). It is a hinge: A's weak break at 6:27/7:1 invites reading the blessing together with the tabernacle's consecration. Flag that connection in pass 1 of U07.
- *Note:* The blessing's later use in liturgy is an intertext-pass lead.

**U07 · 7:1–89 · The chieftains' gifts and the Voice above the cover**
- *Markers:* S after 7:11, then a petuhah after each day's offering (7:17 through 7:83), then after 7:88 and after 7:89.
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Why here:* This is the book's longest chapter and is built on twelve identical paragraphs. 7:1 is a dated flashback to the day the tabernacle was set up, which is earlier than 1:1. 7:89 stands as a single-verse paragraph: Moses hears the Voice from above the cover (*kapporet*) between the cherubim.
- *Note:* Pass 2 should spend its depth on 7:1–11 and 7:89 and treat the repetition structurally, not verse by verse.

**U08 · 8:1–26 · Lamps and Levites**
- *Markers:* P after 8:4 (the lampstand), S after 8:22, P after 8:26.
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Commentators:* Levine, "The Dedication of the Levites."
- *Why here:* The lampstand (8:1–4) and the Levites' purification and wave-offering (8:5–26) are two aspects of service at the sanctuary.

**U09 · 9:1–10:10 · Last orders at Sinai: second Passover, cloud, trumpets**
- *Markers:* P after 9:8, P after 9:14, P after 9:23, P after 10:10. For the break after 9:14, A has only a setumah, which ties the Passover law to the cloud paragraph.
- *Grid:* **Diverges.** The unit crosses the 9|10 chapter break.
- *Commentators:* Levine splits differently: ch. 9 is "Two Matters of Cultic Significance," and 10:1–28 is "On the March." Constable puts all of ch. 10, trumpets included, under "Departure."
- *Why here:* 9:1 is dated, again earlier than 1:1. The petuhah after 10:10, followed by the new date at 10:11, marks 10:1–10 (the silver trumpets) as the last Sinai instruction rather than the first step of the march. The cloud paragraph (9:15–23) is the key presence text; its refrain "at the command of the LORD they camped, at the command of the LORD they set out" is a thread.

#### II. Sinai to Paran (10:11–12:16)

**U10 · 10:11–36 · Setting out**
- *Markers:* L has setumot after each division's departure (10:17, 21, 24, 28), plus S after 10:34 and S after 10:36. The Song of the Ark (10:35–36) is set off in the scroll tradition by two inverted *nun* signs. A has no breaks within the march order and a petuhah after 10:36.
- *Grid:* **Diverges.** The unit starts mid-chapter.
- *Commentators:* Levine splits the unit at 10:28/29 and takes 10:29–12:16 as "Encounters and Experiences." Constable's "Departure" is the whole chapter.
- *Why here:* Narrative logic follows the date at 10:11 and the strong break after 10:36. Hobab (10:29–32) and the ark going ahead (10:33–36) are the last scenes before complaint begins.
- *Note:* Psalm 68:1 reuses the ark saying (10:35–36). This is an intertext-pass lead.

**U11 · 11:1–35 · Craving: fire, quail, and seventy elders**
- *Markers:* P after 11:15, P after 11:22, P after 11:35.
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Why here:* 11:1–3 (Taberah) is the template type-scene for the wilderness complaints: complaint, then fire, then outcry, then intercession, then a place-name. Every later complaint story echoes it.
- *Note:* Both the Exodus 16 manna story and Psalm 78 stand behind this unit. These are intertext-pass leads.

**U12 · 12:1–16 · Miriam, Aaron, and the humble man**
- *Markers:* S after 12:3 (the aside on Moses' humility), P after 12:13 (Moses' five-word prayer), P after 12:16.
- *Grid:* Aligns. English 12:16 is the itinerary notice that closes the unit.

#### III. Kadesh: the refusal and its answer (13:1–15:41)

**U13 · 13:1–14:10 · The spies' report and the people's refusal**
- *Markers:* **None within the unit.** 13:1–14:10 is the longest unbroken stretch in the book outside Balaam. L closes it with a petuhah after 14:10, just as the glory appears at the tent.
- *Grid:* **Diverges.** The unit crosses the 13|14 chapter break.
- *Commentators:* Levine takes 13–14 as one Part. Constable's section runs across chs. 11–20.
- *Why here:* The break sits at the climax, where the people are about to stone Joshua and Caleb and the *kavod* ("glory") intervenes. It separates the human crisis from the divine response.
- *Note:* The report is a type-scene: the spies' majority report set against Joshua and Caleb's dissent.

**U14 · 14:11–45 · Intercession, sentence, and the failed assault**
- *Markers:* P after 14:25, P after 14:45.
- *Grid:* The unit starts mid-chapter.
- *Why here:* This unit is the thematic centre of the book. Moses quotes Exod 34:6–7 back to God (14:18). The forty-years sentence is declared "a year for each day." The unit ends in defeat at Hormah (14:45), which U21 reverses. The Exodus 32–34 intercession is the controlling intertext, and Psalm 95 and Hebrews 3:7–4:11 read this chapter's unbelief later.

**U15 · 15:1–41 · "When you come into the land": offerings, sin, and tassels**
- *Markers:* P after 15:16, S after 15:21, S after 15:26, P after 15:31, S after 15:34, P after 15:36, P after 15:41.
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Commentators:* Levine, "Unfinished Cultic Business."
- *Legal handling — stands alone.* Placement is the argument here. Right after the sentence of death in the wilderness, God speaks as if the land were certain ("when you come," 15:2). The unit contains one narrative (the Sabbath-gatherer, 15:32–36). The tassels law (15:37–41), with its "do not go about after your heart and eyes," picks up the verb *tur* ("scout, spy out") from ch. 13. That link is a thread to name in pass 2.

#### IV. Rebellion and the priesthood (16:1–19:22)

**U16 · 16:1–35 · Korah, Dathan, and Abiram**
- *Markers:* L has petuhot after 16:19, 16:22, and 16:35. A has setumot at all three and no major break until 16:40.
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Commentators:* Levine takes 16–17 as one Part, "The Korah Incident."
- *Note:* Jude 11 reads Korah's rebellion later.

**U17 · 16:36–17:13 [Heb 17:1–28] · Two memorials: bronze plating and the budding staff**
- *Markers:* L has P after 16:43 [Heb 17:8], P after 16:50 [Heb 17:15], S after 17:9 [Heb 17:24], S after 17:11 [Heb 17:26], and S after 17:13 [Heb 17:28]. **A has a petuhah after 16:40 [Heb 17:5] that L lacks**, and a setumah (not a petuhah) after 16:43.
- *Grid:* **Diverges from the English chapter break but matches the Hebrew chapter 17 exactly.**
- *Why here:* The censer plating (a "sign" and "reminder") and Aaron's staff (kept "as a sign," 17:10) are paired objects placed at the sanctuary. Between them comes the plague that Aaron stops by standing "between the dead and the living." The unit ends with the people's cry, "we perish," which ch. 18 answers.
- *Open call:* A's 16:40 break would instead attach the censers to U16.

**U18 · 18:1–32 · Priests and Levites: charge and portion**
- *Markers:* S after 18:7, S after 18:20, P after 18:24, P after 18:32.
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Legal handling — stands alone.* This is the direct answer to 17:12–13: who may approach, and who bears the guilt. Whitt points out that God speaks to Aaron alone here (18:1, 8, 20), which is unique in the Torah. The key line, "I am your portion and your inheritance" (18:20), introduces *nachalah* ("inheritance"), a keyword that grows through Part Two.

**U19 · 19:1–22 · The red cow and the impurity of death**
- *Markers:* P after 18:32, P after 19:22. There are no internal breaks.
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Legal handling — stands alone.* It closes the death-heavy sequence of chs. 16–17 and prepares 20:1 (Miriam's death) and 20:22–29 (Aaron's). Hebrews 9:13 reads it later.

#### V. The fortieth year: Kadesh to the plains of Moab (20:1–22:1)

**U20 · 20:1–13 · Miriam's death and the waters of Meribah**
- *Markers:* P after 20:6, S after 20:11, S after 20:13.
- *Grid:* The unit starts the chapter but ends mid-chapter.
- *Commentators:* Levine takes ch. 20 as one Part, "The End of the Wilderness Period."
- *Why here:* 20:1 is dated, but the year is not given. Only 33:38 shows that ~38 years have passed silently. Exod 17:1–7 is the doublet, and Moses' exclusion is the crux. The Meribah/Massah naming is a thread (Ps 95; 106:32–33).

**U21 · 20:14–21:3 · Edom's refusal, Aaron's death, Hormah reversed**
- *Markers:* P after 20:21, S after 20:29, P after 21:3.
- *Grid:* **Diverges.** The unit crosses the 20|21 chapter break.
- *Why here:* L's markers group Aaron's death with the Arad victory. 21:1–3 reverses 14:45: the place-name Hormah (from *cherem*, "devoted to destruction") marks the first victory of the new era. The brother-language to Edom ("your brother Israel," 20:14) looks back to Jacob and Esau and forward to Obadiah.

**U22 · 21:4–20 · The bronze serpent and the songs of the road**
- *Markers:* S after 21:16, P after 21:20.
- *Grid:* The unit starts mid-chapter.
- *Why here:* This is the last complaint story (21:4–9), followed by an itinerary laced with poetry: the "Book of the Wars of the LORD" (21:14–15) and the well song (21:17–18).
- *Note:* NT reception is central (John 3:14–15), as is later reuse in 2 Kgs 18:4 (Nehushtan).

**U23 · 21:21–22:1 · Sihon and Og; arrival on the plains of Moab**
- *Markers:* There are no breaks from 21:21 until the setumah after 22:1.
- *Grid:* **Diverges.** The unit crosses the 21|22 chapter break.
- *Why here:* The first "plains of Moab" notice (22:1) is the itinerary's goal and closes the movement. The Heshbon taunt-song (21:27–30) has Moabite Stele background (Levine, map 2). Deut 2–3 and Ps 135–136 are later retellings.

#### VI. On the plains of Moab: Balaam and Peor (22:2–25:18)

The Masoretic text treats **22:2–24:25 as a single unbroken open paragraph** (setumah after 22:1, petuhah after 24:25). The five units below divide it for session size. They follow the story's own markers: scene changes, and the oracle formula *wayyissa' meshalo* ("he took up his discourse," 23:7, 18; 24:3, 15, 20, 21, 23). This is one of the two stretches where Rashi gets real weight. Levine takes 22–24 as one Part.

The block's type-scene is the outside prophet compelled to bless (compare Gen 12:3). The New Testament reads Balaam later in 2 Pet 2:15–16, Jude 11, and Rev 2:14; search these in the intertext pass of every unit here, not only where they are noted (U25, U28).

**U24 · 22:2–21 · Balak's summons**
- *Why here:* Two embassies. In both, God says what Balaam may do. The unit ends with Balaam setting out.

**U25 · 22:22–35 · The donkey and the angel**
- *Why here:* The scene turns on sight: the donkey sees what the seer cannot. It is a self-contained comic-ironic scene. 2 Pet 2:15–16 and Jude 11 read it later.

**U26 · 22:36–23:26 · The first and second oracles**
- *Why here:* Oracles 1 and 2 share one structure: altars, offerings, the word placed in the mouth, and Balak's protest. Paired, they read as a repeated type-scene, and the third oracle breaks the pattern.

**U27 · 23:27–24:25 · The third oracle and the star out of Jacob**
- *Why here:* Balaam stops seeking omens, and the spirit comes on him (24:2). The oracle is "How fair are your tents" (24:5); 24:9 quotes Gen 12:3 and 27:29, giving the unit a strong earlier-Scripture root. The fourth oracle then opens with "in the latter days" (24:14) and gives the star and sceptre oracle (24:17) — later reception is heavy: Gen 49:10 behind it, and Qumran, Bar Kokhba, and Matt 2 after it. The short oracles against the nations follow. The petuhah after 24:25 closes the Balaam block.

**U28 · 25:1–26:1a [Heb 25:1–19] · Baal Peor and Phinehas**
- *Markers:* P after 25:9, P after 25:15, P after Heb 25:19.
- *Grid:* **Diverges, mid-verse.** The Hebrew petuhah falls between "And it came to pass after the plague" (Heb 25:19) and the census command (Heb 26:1). English joins them as 26:1. The Masoretic break thus attaches "after the plague" to Peor. No ref-format change: the mid-verse boundary (25:1–26:1a, then 26:1b) is handled by English numbering, which `units-from-map` converts through `versify.py`.
- *Commentators:* Levine takes ch. 25 as one Part.
- *Why here:* Placement against the 26:64–65 verdict is an open question, not a given. Deut 2:14 counts 38 years from Kadesh to the Zered (Num 21:12), "until the entire generation of the men of war had perished," and Balaam (22–24) comes after the Zered. On that reading Peor is the new generation's first sin on the threshold, inside Part One's label (1:1–25:18) but after the verdict's work is done; weigh it in pass 2. The covenant of peace with Phinehas (25:12–13) links to Mal 2:4–5 and Ps 106:30–31, and 1 Cor 10:8 and Rev 2:14 read the episode later. Numbers 31:16 later ties Peor to Balaam retroactively, which is a threads.retro candidate once U33 is walked.

### PART TWO — 26:1–36:13

#### VII. The new generation counted and ordered (26:1–30:16)

**U29 · 26:1b–65 · The second accounting**
- *Markers:* Setumot divide the tribes (26:11 through 26:47), then P after 26:51 (the total), S after 26:56, S after 26:65. Whitt notes the contrast with ch. 1, where the tribes are divided by petuhot.
- *Grid:* Aligns, apart from the 26:1a issue above.
- *Why here:* 26:63–65 closes the census bracket explicitly: "not a man of them was left… except Caleb and Joshua." Rashi has substantive census material here.

**U30 · 27:1–23 · Daughters who inherit, a successor who leads**
- *Markers:* S after 27:5, where Moses brings the case before the LORD; the scroll tradition also enlarges a letter in this verse. S after 27:11. L has a petuhah after 27:14 where A has a setumah. P after 27:23.
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Commentators:* Levine takes ch. 27 as one Part.
- *Why here:* Two continuity problems face the new generation: land passing through daughters, and leadership passing to Joshua. The daughters' case is a type-scene: a legal petition that changes the law. The unit opens an **inclusio with U38** (ch. 36), which brackets the legal material of Part Two. "Like sheep that have no shepherd" (27:17) is heard again in 1 Kgs 22:17, Ezek 34, and Matt 9:36.

**U31 · 28:1–29:40 [Heb 28:1–30:1] · The calendar of public offerings**
- *Markers:* There is a paragraph for each occasion. The main break is P after 28:31, which divides the daily/Sabbath/new-moon/spring festivals from the seventh-month festivals. The unit ends at P after Heb 30:1 (= Eng 29:40).
- *Grid:* Crosses a chapter break by design.
- *Commentators:* Levine takes 28–29 together as one Part.
- *Legal handling — stands alone, one unit.* The calendar is formulaic and set out as lists, so pass 2 should read it structurally, not verse by verse. The new generation receives the full rhythm of worship before entering the land.

**U32 · 30:1–16 [Heb 30:2–17] · Vows: binding words**
- *Markers:* P after Heb 30:1 (= Eng 29:40), P after Heb 30:17 (= Eng 30:16).
- *Grid:* Aligns in English. In Hebrew the chapter starts one verse earlier.
- *Legal handling — stands alone.* Its theme, words that bind and who can annul them, is the same one Balaam's story raised. It also connects forward to Jephthah's vow (Judg 11) and to 1 Sam 1.

#### VIII. Transjordan: war and settlement (31:1–32:42)

**U33 · 31:1–54 · Vengeance on Midian and the division of spoil**
- *Markers:* S after 31:12 ("the plains of Moab"), S after 31:20, P after 31:24, P after 31:54.
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Commentators:* Levine, "The Midianite War." Constable groups chs. 31–32.
- *Why here:* The unit fulfils the command in 25:16–18 and discloses Balaam's role in Peor (31:16).

**U34 · 32:1–42 · Reuben and Gad**
- *Markers:* S after 32:4, S after 32:15, P after 32:19, P after 32:42.
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Why here:* Moses retells the spies' story explicitly (32:8–13), which gives the unit a strong internal echo of U13–U14. It is later taken up in Josh 1:12–18 and 22.

#### IX. Looking back, looking across (33:1–36:13)

**U35 · 33:1–49 · The stages of the journey**
- *Markers:* S after 33:39 (after the dated notice of Aaron's death, 33:38–39), S after 33:49.
- *Grid:* The unit ends mid-chapter.
- *Commentators:* This matches Constable (33:1–49).
- *Why here:* The only list in the book written by Moses (33:2). The route differs from chs. 13–21 (Levine, map 3), which is a tension to hold. Royal itinerary texts are the ANE comparanda.

**U36 · 33:50–34:29 · Dispossession, borders, and the men who divide the land**
- *Markers:* P after 33:56, P after 34:15, P after 34:29. These are three petuhah paragraphs.
- *Grid:* **Diverges.** The unit crosses the 33|34 chapter break.
- *Commentators:* Constable's final section begins at 33:50 too.
- *Why here:* A new "plains of Moab… when you cross the Jordan" speech opens at 33:50. Its three paragraphs are one argument: drive out the inhabitants, here are the borders (Levine, map 4: "greater Canaan"), and here are the men who will allot the land. The warning in 33:55–56 connects to Josh 23:13 and Judg 2:3.

**U37 · 35:1–34 · Levite cities, cities of refuge, and blood that pollutes the land**
- *Markers:* P after 35:8, P after 35:34.
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Legal handling — stands alone.* The two paragraphs are joined by 35:6: the six refuge cities are among the Levites' forty-eight. The unit ends on the presence theme at full strength: "you shall not defile the land… in which I dwell" (35:34). This echoes 5:3, one of the book's largest inclusios. Deut 19 and Josh 20 take it up later.

**U38 · 36:1–13 · Zelophehad's daughters again; colophon**
- *Markers:* There are no internal breaks. The book ends with the colophon at 36:13, which contains the final "plains of Moab" notice.
- *Grid:* Aligns.
- *Why here:* This closes the inclusio with U30 and repeats its type-scene (a second legal petition changes the law again), and inheritance (*nachalah*) gets the book's last word. Josh 17:3–6 carries the story forward.
