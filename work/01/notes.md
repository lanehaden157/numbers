# Unit 1 notes: Numbers 1:1-54, the first accounting (full tier)

Words: `kit.py words 1:1-54` (VERIFIED = the Hebrew transliteration read there; Num 16:2, 16:46, 18:5, 26:2, 26:9, 10:17 read the same way). Other books' Hebrew is read as pack leads (SHEET) or in English (ENGLISH). The Greek of Num 1 read with `lxx --expect`. Hebrew numbering = English numbering in this unit.

## The scene and its parts

People: Yahweh, Moshe, Aharon (named in 1:3 and 1:17 and 1:44; see the tension below), twelve chieftains (nesiʾim) each named with his father, the whole community (ʿedah), the Levites. Places: the wilderness of Sinai; the Tent of Meeting; the Dwelling of the Testimony (mishkan ha-ʿedut). Objects: the Dwelling and its vessels. Dated 1:1: first day of the second month, second year, a month after the Dwelling was raised (Exod 40:17, English); nineteen days before the cloud lifts on the twentieth (Num 10:11, English).

Shape: a command (1:1-4), the twelve names (1:5-16), carrying out (1:17-19), twelve tribal entries in one frame, summed (1:20-46), then the Levites set apart and the camp described from the Dwelling out (1:47-53), and a compliance line (1:54). Masoretic breaks (pack): a petuhah after 1:19 and after each tribe through 1:43, then after 1:47 and 1:54. Whitt's note marks the first of these (after the opening paragraph, near 1:19) as a Leningrad petuhah; the map says Aleppo has a setumah there.

The twelve entries run: Reuven, Shimon, Gad (the camp of Reuven in ch. 2), Yehudah, Yissakhar, Zevulun (Yehudah's camp), Ephrayim, Menashsheh, Binyamin (Ephrayim's camp), Dan, Asher, Naphtali (Dan's camp). The chieftain list (1:5-15) puts Gad eleventh, after Asher. Both orders start with Reuven, the firstborn; Levine says the Reuven-first order is shared with chs. 13 and 26, and Yehudah-first with chs. 2, 7, 10, 34.

Nearest same-shape passage: Num 26:1-65 (second census; read the English and the Hebrew of 26:2, 26:9). Same: command to lift the head of the whole community, twenty and above, all who go out to the army (26:2 repeats the 1:2-3 wording word for word); a tribe-by-tribe list; a sum (26:51: 601,730); the Levites counted apart (26:57-62). Different: 26:2 gives no "by the number of names"; the chieftain team is replaced by Eleazar; 26:64-65 says no one counted in Sinai survives except Caleb and Yehoshua. Does: ch. 1 sets the account that ch. 26 closes.

## Census totals: what each commentator's own lines say (lesson: read their lines, not each other's reports)

The question: Exod 38:26 gives 603,550 for the shekel count about six months earlier and Num 1:46 gives the same 603,550. Is the Numbers count the same count? Is it the same men?

- Rashi on 1:2 (own line): "by their polls" means "by means of shekels, a beka a head", as at Exod 38:26. Rashi has no comment on 1:46 and none on 1:20-45. Rashi on 1:18: they brought genealogical records and witnesses.
- Ramban on 1:3 (own lines): paqad means attention and supervision, "in all places without exception"; so tiphkedu otham means Moshe was not to count heads but to supervise the half-shekels, as in David's miphkad. On 1:18 he rejects Rashi's documents and witnesses: va-yityaldu means each declared his family and tribe while bringing the half-shekel. He reports Ibn Ezra's birth-date reading without comment.
- Ibn Ezra on 1:2 and 1:18 (own lines): on 1:2 he refers to Exod 30:12 for "lift the head" and does not mention shekels in Numbers. On 1:18 va-yityaldu means they gave their birth dates, because only those twenty and over were counted (a note adds a manuscript reading: "their children were recorded"). Bahya on 1:18 attributes to Ibn Ezra the view that each recorded his genealogy in writing: that is not what Ibn Ezra's own line says in this edition; use Ibn Ezra's line. I have not read Ibn Ezra on Exod 38:26 or Exod 30:12 (not in the source set), so what Ibn Ezra thinks of the identical total is unavailable.
- Rabbeinu Bahya on 1:3 (own line): they got the number through the half-shekel each paid, not by counting heads.
- Or HaChaim on 1:2 (own lines): the identical total is a problem, because six months of growth should show. He solves it from Shekalim 1:3: the Levites were in the Exodus count but not here, so the growth equals the Levites. He also reports Pesikta Zutra, that no one died while they worked on the Dwelling.
- Ellicott on 1:3: the earlier count was probably made by the half-shekels, as Ithamar did, and "exactly corresponds" to this one. Pulpit on 1:2 and 1:46: the shekel poll anticipated the numbers; the adult male Levites about 10,000, so growth of about 13,000. Henry on 1:44-46: some think the Levites were in the Exodus total.
- Levine on 1:20-43: the totals' historical significance is exaggerated; the 600,000 comes from a sexagesimal system and matches the JE figure (Exod 12:37, Num 11:21); ʾelep here means one thousand, against Mendenhall's clan reading. He does not treat Exod 38:26. Constable: each figure ends in zero, so the numbers look rounded; he reads ʾelep as thousands. Doty: defends the figures against Butzer and Gray (see below).

Where they split: (a) was the Numbers count made through the shekels (Rashi, Ramban, Bahya; Ibn Ezra's Numbers lines are silent)? (b) what does va-yityaldu require (Rashi: documents and witnesses; Ramban: a declaration with the shekel; Ibn Ezra: birth dates)? (c) why the identical total (Or HaChaim's Levite arithmetic; Henry and Pulpit's version)? The text itself: 1:3 and 1:20-43 name no shekel; 1:2 and 1:18 say "by the number of names" and "by their heads".

## Tensions and field calls to keep open (told, not resolved)

- "you and Aharon" (1:3) is a gloss in Levine and an addition of Whitt's fifth stage; 1:19 says "he mustered them" (singular verb), but 1:17 and 1:44 say Moshe and Aharon. VERIFIED: 1:19 wa-yyipqedem is singular.
- The Tent: Levine (1:1 note) hears two Priestly and non-Priestly conceptions of the Tent of Meeting, centre of the camp with sacrifice (P) against an oracle tent outside the camp (Exod 33:6-11), "not to be synthesised". Whitt adds that Yahweh is already speaking in the Tent before it is consecrated later in Numbers, and that the mountain itself goes unmentioned. Whitt's reading is composition history; Ramban's is literary: Numbers repeats "from the Tent" because Lev 25:1 had said "Mount Sinai".
- Whitt on 1:1: the "their departure" has no antecedent, which he reads as showing Numbers once ran on from Exodus on one scroll: inference.
- The meaning of paqad: Levine (basic sense "hand over, assign, attend to"; counting is derived; pequddim mostly "musters" not "totals") against Ramban (attention and supervision everywhere). They agree that "count" is not the root sense.
- The Levites: Ibn Ezra and Rashbam (never military, charged with the Dwelling); Ramban (Moshe left them out of his own accord because no chieftain was named for Levi; God then explains in 1:49); Sforno (they waited for instructions); Bahya (set apart because of rank, not ineligibility; midrash ties it to the calf, as does Doty: "may have stemmed in part"). Levine: policy of Numbers, anticipating chs. 3-4 and 8.
- The stranger: Rashi, put to death by heaven, not by a court (Sanhedrin 84a); Ibn Ezra, a court puts him to death; Whitt, any non-Levite; Levine (after Milgrom 1970) capital punishment for violating sacred space, here unconsecrated Israelites and non-Israelites. 18:22 backs the wider reading.
- The wrath (1:53): Levine, danger of divine wrath after intrusion or neglect; Ibn Ezra, no one from Israel is struck (as with Uzzah), "upon the community" meaning "upon anyone"; Rashi, there will be wrath if outsiders share the Levites' service, as at Qorach; Whitt, defilement of the shrine causing Yahweh's anger.
- Doty's numerical defence: Gray's Num 3:43 arithmetic (22,273 firstborn, so 2 to 2.5 million people) is unit 3's problem; carry forward. Whitt: the army scheme is a late portrait, tied to the conquest idea in Joshua.
- Ahira (1:15): Doty reads an Egyptian Ra in the name; Levine reads ra as "friend" (my brother is a friend). Doty's "half the 24 names carry El or Shaddai": my own count of the table gives twelve (nine with El: Elitsur, Shelumiel, Nethanel, Eliab, Elishama, Gamliel, Pagiel, Elyasaph, Deuel; three with Shaddai: Shedeur, Tsurishaddai, Ammishaddai). Levine: names found in Numbers 1, 2, 7, 10 only, "more traditional than historical".
- Who counts: Pulpit on 1:18 says the census was done in one day; Ramban on 1:18 says it began that day and was not finished that day.

## Thread opens (popover notes)

All 15 threads open here and match the pack (no mismatch to report). Draft notes, one line each:

- account (paqad, 1:3): the verb that runs the two censuses (chs. 1, 26) and the Levite counts; Levine: root sense is "attend to, assign". Later "call to account" (14:18, 29).
- community (ʿedah, 1:2): Levine's "community", a social body (Ugaritic, Elephantine); the book's word for Israel gathered.
- carry (nasaʾ, 1:2): "lift the head" opens it; later the Levites "carry" the Dwelling (1:50) and Yahweh's burden on Moshe (ch. 11).
- number (mispar, 1:2): "by the number of names", heads counted one by one.
- army (tsabaʾ, 1:3): the Exodus armies (Exod 12:41) mustered; Levine: also "service" (Num 4:3, 8:24).
- tribe-staff (matteh, 1:4): the priestly word for tribe, a staff.
- chieftain (nasiʾ, 1:16): one "lifted up" to lead; the twelve heads of houses; the same title for Qorach's 250 chiefs.
- firstborn (bekor, 1:20): Reuven "firstborn of Yisrael", demoted in Gen 49:3-4; the Levite-for-firstborn exchange comes in ch. 3.
- camp (maḥaneh, 1:50): the Levites encamp around the Dwelling; chapter 2 draws the camp.
- set out (nasaʿ, 1:51): the Levites take the Dwelling down "when it sets out": the departure formula of chs. 10 and 33.
- stranger (zar, 1:51): the unauthorized person who comes near the Dwelling dies.
- near (qarab, 1:51): "come near", the book's other danger word; 18:22 widens it to all Israelites.
- company (degel, 1:52): "each by his company", the tribal unit camped under a standard.
- wrath (qetsep, 1:53): wrath as the result of intrusion; 18:5 repeats the clause.
- charge (mishmeret, 1:53): the Levites' duty of care; Levine: a duty, not simply a guard post.
