The Book of Numbers · Study Translation

The Camp around the Tent

Unit 2 · Numbers 2:1–34

    Camp by your companies · 2:1–2

    1 Yahweh spoke to Moshe and to Aharon, saying:

    and to Aharon — chapter 1’s orders went to Moshe; the plan that centres on the sanctuary is given to the priest as well.

    2 “Each man by his company, with the signs of their fathers’ house, Yisrael’s sons shall camp; facing, at a distance, all around the Tent of Meeting they shall camp.1

    facing, at a distance — the camp looks toward the tent across open ground, with the Levites between (1:53).

    East: the camp of Yehudah · 2:3–9

    3 Those camping frontward, toward the sunrise: the company of the camp of Yehudah, by their armies; and the chieftain of the sons of Yehudah, Nachshon son of Amminadav.2

    frontward, toward the sunrise — east is the front, the side the Dwelling’s entrance faces.
    Exod 27:13; 38:13 — the same doubled phrase, “frontward, toward the sunrise,” names the east side of the Dwelling’s court, where its entrance stands. Yehudah camps on the side named in the sanctuary’s own words.
    1 Chr 2:10 — Nachshon is again “chieftain of the sons of Yehudah,” the same title, now set inside the line that runs through Boaz to David.

    4 His army, and their accounted ones: seventy-four thousand six hundred.

    5 And those camping next to him: the tribe of Yissakhar; and the chieftain of the sons of Yissakhar, Netanel son of Tsuar.

    6 His army, and its accounted ones: fifty-four thousand four hundred.

    7 The tribe of Zevulun; and the chieftain of the sons of Zevulun, Eliav son of Chelon.

    8 His army, and its accounted ones: fifty-seven thousand four hundred.

    9 All the accounted ones of the camp of Yehudah: one hundred eighty-six thousand four hundred, by their armies. First they shall set out.

    the pattern — the south, west and north camps repeat vv3–9; their tribe-by-tribe lines are set out as tables below.
    Gen 49:3–4, 8–10 — Reuven the firstborn loses preeminence and the sceptre is promised to Yehudah; 1 Chr 5:1–2 says the leader came from Yehudah. Here Yehudah sets out first.

    South: the camp of Reuven · 2:10–16

    10 The company of the camp of Reuven, southward, by their armies; and the chieftain of the sons of Reuven, Elitsur son of Shedeur.

    southward — literally “to the right,” reckoned by someone facing east.

    The tent in the middle · 2:17

    17 Then the Tent of Meeting shall set out, the camp of the Levites, in the midst of the camps. As they camp, so they shall set out, each man at his station, by their companies.4

    at his station — literally “at his hand,” the place within one’s reach. Two camps precede this verse and two follow it, as in the march.
    Exod 25:8 promised, “I will dwell in their midst”; after the calf the tent stood “outside the camp, far off” (Exod 33:7). Now it sets out in the midst of the camps.

    West: the camp of Ephrayim · 2:18–24

    18 The company of the camp of Ephrayim, by their armies, seaward; and the chieftain of the sons of Ephrayim, Elishama son of Ammihud.5

    seaward — the west, named for the sea as it lies from the land of Canaan.
    Ps 80:1–2 — “enthroned on the cherubim… before Ephrayim and Binyamin and Menasheh, stir up your might”: the very triad that marches directly behind the sanctuary.

    The sum, and Levi apart · 2:32–34

    32 These are the accounted ones of Yisrael’s sons by their fathers’ house; all the accounted ones of the camps, by their armies: six hundred three thousand five hundred fifty.6

    603,550 — the four camp totals sum to the figure of 1:46.

    33 But the Levites were not accounted among Yisrael’s sons, as Yahweh commanded Moshe.7

    not accounted — the tribe nearest the presence stands outside the army’s count.

    34 And Yisrael’s sons did according to all that Yahweh commanded Moshe: so they camped by their companies, and so they set out, each man by his clans, by his fathers’ house.8

    so they camped… so they set out — the command is reported as already done; chapter 10 narrates the first march.

    Notes

    1. company, signs, at a distance (v2). Three words carry the verse. Company (degel) is traditionally rendered “banner,” but the verse keeps a separate word for the visible signs (ʾotot), and degel names garrison companies in Persian-period Aramaic papyri from Elephantine and ostraca from Arad; the translation follows that reading, the company camped under a flag. The root means “look at”: its verb gives Ps 20:5 (“we lift our banners,” or “we look forward”) and Song 6:4, 10 (“awesome as the bannered ones”), and the Qumran War Scroll reuses the whole camp-and-banner order. Later tradition gave the four lead camps the emblems of man, lion, ox and eagle, the four faces of the living creatures (Ezek 1:10; Rev 4:7). Signs is the word for the lights of Gen 1:14 and the wonders Yisrael will not trust (14:11); here the signs have been read as coloured cloths matching each tribe’s stone on the high priest’s breastpiece (Exod 28:21), or as the places Yaakov assigned his sons around his bier (Gen 50:12–13). Facing, at a distance (mineged) is the word for Hagar sitting “a bowshot off” (Gen 21:16) and for Moshe seeing the land he will not enter (Deut 32:52). One tradition sets the gap at two thousand cubits, by way of the space kept from the chest in Josh 3:4.
    2. Nachshon son of Amminadav (v3). Aharon married Nachshon’s sister Elisheva (Exod 6:23), so the priest camped at the front of the Dwelling (3:38) and the chieftain camped at the front of the tribes are brothers-in-law. The east side of the camp is one family. “Frontward” (qedmah) shares its root with the “east” where the garden was planted and where the cherubim were stationed (Gen 2:8; 3:24); some readers hear the camp’s eastward face as recalling that garden. It is a reading worth weighing, not a stated link.
    3. Reuven’s camp (vv10–16). Reuven and Shimon are Leah’s first sons; Gad, the firstborn of her maid Zilpah (Gen 30:9–11), takes the place Levi would otherwise hold. Two explanations are offered: Gad is the nearest substitute within Leah’s household, or Gad and Reuven belong together because they will settle side by side east of the Jordan (ch. 32). Gad’s chieftain is “Elyasaph son of Reuel” here but “son of Deuel” at 1:14, 7:42, 47 and 10:20; the two Hebrew letters are easily confused, and the ancient witnesses divide. The text is translated as it stands.
    4. in the midst of the camps (v17). The verse stands as its own paragraph in the Hebrew text, between the first two camps and the last two, so the page enacts the march. “In the midst” is presence language, and the book returns to it: the camp must be kept clean because Yahweh dwells in its midst (5:3), and so must the land (35:34). Chapter 10 complicates the picture. The frame of the Dwelling goes ahead with Yehudah’s camp and the holy things follow Reuven’s (10:17–21), and the chest travels three days ahead (10:33). Interpreters have long disagreed whether the march moved as a square or a single line. Egyptian reliefs of Ramesses II’s war camp put Pharaoh’s tent at the centre of a rectangle facing east; Greek descriptions of the Persian king’s camp put his east-facing tent in the middle of an inner ring of attendants and an outer ring of troops, each man knowing his place. Either way, the centre here belongs to Yahweh, not a king. The picture is drawn again after the exile, with the tribes around the sanctuary and twelve gates, three to a side (Ezek 48:31–34), and in the New Testament: the Word “tented” among us (John 1:14), the tent of God is with humans, and the city has twelve gates named for the tribes (Rev 21:3, 12–13).
    5. Ephrayim before Menasheh (v18). Ephrayim, the younger, is listed first, as Yaakov set him when he crossed his hands (Gen 48:14–20). With Yehudah ahead of Reuven (v3), the chapter holds two instances of the younger son preferred. Ephrayim’s chieftain, Elishama son of Ammihud, may be the man named in 1 Chr 7:26–27, where Ammihud and Elishama stand in the line that leads to Yehoshua. If so, the first generation’s chieftain is grandfather to the man who will lead the second into the land.
    6. six hundred three thousand five hundred fifty (v32). The four camp totals (186,400; 151,450; 108,100; 157,600) sum to the total of 1:46. How the totals should be read is discussed at 1:46; the arrangement here does not change that question.
    7. were not accounted (v33). The verb is a rare passive-reflexive form of the account root, repeating the exemption of 1:47. The same form marks the Levites’ exemption from the second count (26:62). Its one use outside Numbers describes Yisrael’s army “accounted” and camped opposite the Arameans (1 Kgs 20:27).
    8. did according to all (v34). The compliance formula is the one that closed the building of the Dwelling (Exod 39:32, 42–43; 40:16): the camp is set up the way the sanctuary was. The verbs change from command (“shall camp, shall set out”) to report (“so they camped, so they set out”), telling the reader the order became habit. The first actual march is narrated at 10:11–28.
