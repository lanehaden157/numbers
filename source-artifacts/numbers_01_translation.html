The Book of Numbers · Study Translation

The First Accounting

Unit 1 · Numbers 1:1–54

    Yahweh orders an accounting · 1:1–4

    1 Yahweh spoke to Moshe in the wilderness of Sinai, in the Tent of Meeting, on the first of the second month, in the second year of their going out from the land of Egypt, saying:

    Dated, and spoken from the Tent: the book opens a month after the Dwelling was raised.
    Exod 40:2, 17, 34–35 — the Dwelling was raised on the first of the first month, and the cloud filled it so that Moshe could not enter. Here, a month later, the first speech of Numbers comes from the Tent; the cloud lifts on the twentieth of this same month (10:11).
    Exod 19:1–2 — the people arrived at “the wilderness of Sinai” and camped. The phrase brackets the whole stay, until they leave it at 10:12; this unit is its last chapter-long scene.

    2 “Lift the head of the whole community of Yisrael’s sons, by their clans, by their fathers’ house, by the number of names, every male, by their skulls.1

    lift the head — the idiom for taking a sum; by their skulls, head by head.
    Exod 30:12 — “When you lift the head of Yisrael’s sons to muster them, each gives a ransom for his life so that no plague strikes them when they are mustered.” Counting is dangerous before a single number is given; there a payment meets the danger, here the Levites’ ring does (1:53).

    3 From twenty years old and up, everyone going out to the army in Yisrael — y’all shall take account of them by their armies, you and Aharon.2

    paqad, “take account of”: to attend to, to register, and later to call to account.
    Exod 6:26; 12:41 — Yahweh brought Yisrael out “by their armies,” and all his armies went out of Egypt on that day. The people who left as armies are now mustered as armies.
    Num 26:2 — the second census opens with this command almost word for word. The two commands are the book’s brackets.

    4 And with you there shall be a man, a man for each tribe, each man the head of his fathers’ house.3

    The twelve who stand with Moshe · 1:5–16

    5 And these are the names of the men who shall stand with you: for Reuven, Elitsur son of Shedeur;

    6 for Shimon, Shelumiel son of Tsurishaddai;

    7 for Yehudah, Nachshon son of Amminadab;

    Exod 6:23 — Nachshon son of Amminadab is the brother of Aharon’s wife Elisheva. Ruth 4:20 and 1 Chr 2:10–11 carry him into David’s line, and Matt 1:4 into Jesus’ genealogy.

    8 for Yissakhar, Netanel son of Tsuar;

    9 for Zevulun, Eliab son of Chelon;

    10 for the sons of Yoseph: for Ephrayim, Elishama son of Ammihud; for Menashsheh, Gamliel son of Pedahtsur;

    Gen 48:5 — the dying Yisrael takes Yoseph’s two sons as his own, “as Reuven and Shimon are mine.” That is why Yoseph’s one place is two here, keeping the tribes at twelve without Levi.

    11 for Binyamin, Abidan son of Gidoni;

    12 for Dan, Achiezer son of Ammishaddai;

    13 for Asher, Pagiel son of Ochran;

    14 for Gad, Elyasaph son of Deuel;4

    15 for Naphtali, Achira son of Enan.”

    16 These are the called of the community, chieftains of their fathers’ tribes; they are the heads of the thousands of Yisrael.5

    nasiʾ, “chieftain,” is literally “one lifted up”: the counting verb names the leaders too.
    Num 16:2; 26:9 — “called of the community” returns for the 250 chiefs who rise with Qorach and for Datan and Abiram. The honourable title of the twelve becomes the title of the rebels.

    The accounting · 1:17–46

    17 And Moshe and Aharon took these men, who had been designated by name,

    18 and they assembled the whole community on the first of the second month, and they registered their descent by their clans, by their fathers’ house, by the number of names, from twenty years old and up, by their skulls,6

    19 as Yahweh commanded Moshe; and he took account of them in the wilderness of Sinai.

    Verses 20–43 repeat one formula twelve times; the table condenses them, with each tribe’s count as the text gives it.

    45 And all the accounted ones of Yisrael’s sons by their fathers’ house, from twenty years old and up, everyone going out to the army in Yisrael —

    46 all the accounted ones were six hundred thousand and three thousand and five hundred and fifty.7

    603,550, stated as the text gives it; how to read “thousand” here is a real question (note 7).
    Exod 12:37; Num 11:21 — about six hundred thousand men on foot left Egypt. The exact list here is the same round number, made exact.
    Num 26:51, 64–65 — the second census gives 601,730 and says that of those counted at Sinai no one is left but Kalev and Yehoshua.

    Levi is set apart · 1:47–51

    47 But the Levites, by their fathers’ tribe, were not accounted among them.8

    Num 26:62 — the Levites are again counted apart, “for no inheritance was given them among Yisrael’s sons.”

    48 Yahweh spoke to Moshe, saying:

    49 “Only the tribe of Levi you shall not take account of, and their head you shall not lift among Yisrael’s sons.

    50 And you — appoint the Levites over the Dwelling of the Testimony, and over all its vessels, and over all that belongs to it. They shall carry the Dwelling and all its vessels, and they shall minister to it, and around the Dwelling they shall camp.9

    The same paqad that counts the tribes now puts the uncounted tribe in charge.
    Exod 38:21 — “the accounts of the Dwelling, the Dwelling of the Testimony, which were reckoned at Moshe’s command, the service of the Levites.” The Levites’ part in the Dwelling was already written down as an account.

    51 When the Dwelling sets out, the Levites shall take it down; when the Dwelling camps, the Levites shall set it up; and the stranger who comes near shall be put to death.10

    zar, “stranger”: anyone not authorized for this holy work.
    Num 3:10; 18:22 — the line returns: only Aharon and his sons keep the priesthood, and at 18:22 the Israelites themselves may not come near the Tent “lest they bear sin and die.”
    Num 10:17 — when the cloud finally lifts, “the Dwelling was taken down” and Gershon and Merari carry it.

    The ring around the Dwelling · 1:52–54

    52 And Yisrael’s sons shall camp, each man by his camp and each man by his company, by their armies.

    53 And the Levites shall camp around the Dwelling of the Testimony, so that there be no wrath on the community of Yisrael’s sons; and the Levites shall keep the charge of the Dwelling of the Testimony.”11

    The Levites stand between the people and the presence: a living buffer, not a fence.
    Num 18:5 — “keep the charge of the holy place and the charge of the altar, that there be no more wrath on Yisrael’s sons.” The clause repeats here nearly word for word.

    54 And Yisrael’s sons did according to all that Yahweh commanded Moshe; so they did.

    Exod 39:32; 40:16 — the same line closed the building of the Dwelling. Here it closes the first order given from it.

    Notes

    1. lift the head (v2). The idiom takes a sum of persons, and it already stood in Exod 30:12, where each one counted pays a ransom. “Take a count” would read more smoothly but hide that link. Skulls keeps the Hebrew’s own picture: a head, one by one.
    2. take account of (v3). The verb paqad has a root sense of attending to or assigning, and counting is one use of it. Some read it as supervision in every use, and take the count here to have been made by overseeing the half-shekels of Exod 30. Verse 3 and verses 20–43 name no shekel; the phrases are “by the number of names” and “by their skulls.” “You and Aharon” may be an explanatory addition: verse 19 has a singular verb, “he took account of them,” while verses 17 and 44 name both men.
    3. tribe (v4). The word, maṭṭeh, is literally “staff.” The tribe is named by its leader’s rod, which matters when twelve staffs are laid before the presence in chapter 17.
    4. Deuel (v14). Translated as written; the same man is Reuel at 2:14, and the better reading is probably Reuel. The two letters differ only by a small stroke, a common scribal confusion.
    5. thousands, called (v16). ʾElef here may mean “a thousand,” as the numerals that follow require, or an older social unit, a clan; “heads of the clans of Yisrael” is the alternative. The written text and the reading tradition differ by one vowel-letter in “called,” without change of sense. One medieval reading takes the phrase to mean that the community acts only when summoned.
    6. registered their descent (v18). A reflexive form of “bear, beget” found only here; the later census (ch. 26) does not use it. Readings differ on what it asks: that each man bring records and witnesses; that he declare his family and tribe when he brought his half-shekel; or that he give his birth date, to show he was twenty. “Registered their descent” keeps the root and stays neutral between them.
    7. six hundred thousand (v46). Exod 38:26 gives the identical total, 603,550, for the half-shekel count taken some months earlier. Some conclude that this count was made through the shekels; some, that the total is the same because the Levites were inside the earlier count and are outside this one; others that the exact sum is a standard figure. The text gives no date for the earlier count and names no shekel here. As to size, taken at face value 603,550 fighting men implies a people of perhaps two million. Some read ʾelef as a clan or military unit, which would make the totals far smaller; others hold to “thousand” and defend the figures as they stand. Every tribal figure ends in zero, which some take as rounding. The translation gives the numbers as the text does. Chapter 26 returns to the same question.
    8. not accounted (v47). Why the Levites are outside the count is explained in several ways: they were never a military tribe, being charged with the Dwelling; they were left out by Moshe because no chieftain was named for Levi, and verse 49 then states the rule; they waited for instruction; or the exemption marks rank, not ineligibility. The text itself gives only the command of verses 49–50.
    9. appoint (v50). The verb is the one that runs through the chapter, paqad. Verses 47 and 49 say the Levites are not “accounted”; verse 50 puts the same root in the causative: they are “appointed over.” English can keep the pun only by changing verbs; the Greek loses it, since there the verb for appoint is a different word from the one in verse 3.
    10. stranger (v51). The same word, zar, bars the outsider from the anointing oil (Exod 30:33) and from holy food (Lev 22:10). It may mean any non-Levite, or unconsecrated Israelites and non-Israelites alike; 18:22 supports the wide reading. Who carries out the sentence is read differently: heaven, or a court. And verse 51 puts all the handling on the Levites, while chapter 4 has Aharon and his sons first take down the veil and cover the holy things, so that Qohath may not touch them.
    11. wrath, charge (v53). The clause echoes the purpose given for the Levites at 8:19, “that there be no plague among Yisrael’s sons when they come near the sanctuary.” One reading takes the wrath as striking any single Israelite, as when Uzza died beside the chest (2 Sam 6:7). Mishmeret is “charge” here: a duty to keep, the word that structures chapters 3–4 and 18. Some take it as guard duty, a night patrol, and the Greek supports that sense with a word for guard.
