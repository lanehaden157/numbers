The Book of Numbers · Study Translation

The First Accounting

Unit 1 · Numbers 1:1–54

    Yahweh orders an accounting · 1:1–16

    1 Yahweh spoke to Moshe in the wilderness of Sinai, in the Tent of Meeting, on the first of the second month, in the second year of their going out from the land of Egypt, saying:

    Dated, and spoken from the tent: the book opens at the place of presence, a month after the Dwelling was raised (Exod 40:17).

    2 “Lift the head of the whole community of Yisrael’s sons, by their clans, by their fathers’ house, by the number of names, every male, by their skulls.1

    lift the head (seʾu et-roʾsh) — the idiom for taking a sum; by their skulls, head by head.
    Exod 30:12 uses the same idiom: when you lift the head of Yisrael’s sons to take account of them, each gives a ransom (kofer) for his life, so that no plague (negef) strikes them. Counting is dangerous before a single number is given; there a payment meets the danger, here the Levites’ ring does (1:53).

    3 From twenty years old and up, everyone going out to the army in Yisrael — y’all shall take account of them by their armies, you and Aharon.2

    paqad, “take account of”: to register, to attend to, and later to call to account.
    Exod 12:41: on that very day all the armies (tsivʾot) of Yahweh went out from the land of Egypt; Exod 6:26 had already said Yahweh would bring them out “by their armies.” The people who went out of Egypt as an army are now counted as one.

    4 And with you there shall be a man, a man for each tribe, each man the head of his fathers’ house.3

    5 And these are the names of the men who shall stand with you: for Reuven, Elitsur son of Shedeur;

    Gen 35:23–26 lists Yisrael’s twelve sons by their mothers: Leah’s six, Rachel’s two, Bilhah’s two, Zilpah’s two. The same names stand here, but Levi has dropped out and Yoseph has become two, and the twelve hold.
    Gen 49:4: Reuven, the firstborn, unstable as water, “you shall not excel.” He still heads the list here, and again at 1:20, but first in order is not first in rank or size; 1 Chr 5:2 says Yehudah grew mighty while the birthright went to Yoseph.

    6 for Shimon, Shelumiel son of Tsurishaddai;

    7 for Yehudah, Nachshon son of Amminadav;

    8 for Yissakhar, Netanel son of Tsuar;

    9 for Zevulun, Eliav son of Chelon;

    10 for the sons of Yoseph: for Ephrayim, Elishama son of Ammihud; for Menashsheh, Gamliel son of Pedahtsur;4

    Gen 48:5: the dying Yisrael takes Yoseph’s two sons as his own — “Ephrayim and Menashsheh shall be mine, as Reuven and Shimon are.” That adoption is why Yoseph’s one place is two here, keeping the tribes at twelve without Levi.

    11 for Binyamin, Avidan son of Gidoni;

    12 for Dan, Achiezer son of Ammishaddai;

    13 for Asher, Pagiel son of Okhran;

    14 for Gad, Elyasaph son of Deuel;

    15 for Naphtali, Achira son of Enan.”

    16 These are the called of the community, chieftains of their fathers’ tribes; they are the heads of the thousands of Yisrael.5

    nasiʾ, “chieftain,” is literally “one lifted up”: the counting verb names the leaders too.

    The accounting · 1:17–47

    17 And Moshe and Aharon took these men, who had been designated by names,6

    18 and they assembled the whole community on the first of the second month, and they declared their descent by their clans, by their fathers’ house, by the number of names, from twenty years old and up, by their skulls,7

    19 as Yahweh commanded Moshe; and he took account of them in the wilderness of Sinai.

    Verses 20–43 repeat one formula twelve times; the table condenses them, with each tribe’s count as the text gives it.

    45 And all the accounted ones of Yisrael’s sons by their fathers’ house, from twenty years old and up, everyone going out to the army in Yisrael —

    46 all the accounted ones were six hundred thousand and three thousand and five hundred and fifty.9

    603,550, stated as the text gives it; how to read “thousand” here is a real question (note 9).
    Exod 38:26 gives the identical total, 603,550, for those twenty and up who paid the half-shekel per skull. There the count funds the Dwelling; here it musters the army that will march around it.
    Isa 40:26: Yahweh “brings out their army (tsevaʾ) by number (mispar), calling them all by name (shem)” — the stars; Ps 147:4 likewise counts a number for the stars and names them all. The same words for the heavenly host set a count by name beside this count by skull.

    47 But the Levites, by their fathers’ tribe, were not accounted among them.

    Levi is not accounted · 1:48–51

    48 Yahweh spoke to Moshe, saying:

    49 “Only the tribe of Levi you shall not take account of, and their head you shall not lift among Yisrael’s sons.10

    David’s census 2 Sam 24:1–15

    Yahweh’s anger burns against Yisrael again, and David is incited to count the people. He sends Yoav through all the tribes, from Dan to Beer-sheva, to take account (paqad) of the people so that he may know their number (mispar); Yoav asks why, and the king overrules him. After nine months and twenty days Yoav reports 800,000 men who draw the sword in Yisrael and 500,000 in Yehudah. David’s heart strikes him and he confesses a great sin; offered famine, flight or plague, he takes the plague, and seventy thousand die.

    • the verb paqad and the noun mispar for the count
    • a count of fighting men, tribe by tribe
    • a leader who orders the count
    • danger, then death, attached to counting

    Here Yahweh commands the count and spells out who is not to be counted; there the count itself is the sin, no tribe is held back, and plague follows.

    It shows what the Levites’ exemption and their ring around the Dwelling are guarding against: the danger of counting named at Exod 30:12 is the one that falls on David’s Yisrael.

    Chronicles recalls that David did not take the number of those under twenty, and that wrath (qetsef), the word of 1:53, came on Yisrael for the count (1 Chr 27:23–24).

    50 And you — appoint the Levites over the Dwelling of the Testimony, and over all its vessels, and over all that belongs to it. They shall carry the Dwelling and all its vessels, and they shall minister to it, and around the Dwelling they shall camp.

    The same paqad that counts the tribes now puts the uncounted tribe in charge.

    51 When the Dwelling sets out, the Levites shall take it down; when the Dwelling camps, the Levites shall set it up; and the stranger who comes near shall be put to death.11

    zar, “stranger”: not a foreigner but anyone not authorized for this holy work, Israelites included.

    The ring around the Dwelling · 1:52–54

    52 And Yisrael’s sons shall camp, each man by his camp and each man by his company, by their armies.

    53 And the Levites shall camp around the Dwelling of the Testimony, so that there be no wrath on the community of Yisrael’s sons; and the Levites shall keep the charge of the Dwelling of the Testimony.”12

    The Levites stand between the people and the presence: a living buffer, not a fence.
    Josh 22:20: when Akhan broke faith over the devoted things, wrath (qetsef) fell on the whole community (ʿedah) of Yisrael. Same word, same community: one man’s trespass reaches all of them, which is exactly what the ring is set to prevent.

    54 And Yisrael’s sons did according to all that Yahweh commanded Moshe; so they did.

    The book’s first notice of obedience.

    Notes

    1. fathers’ house (v2). The count nests three kin-units: tribe, “fathers’ house” (a mid-level lineage), and clan. How the fathers’ house worked socially is debated. The registers of neighbouring peoples used the same pair of ideas, a word for troops and a verb for overseeing and enrolling them.
    2. in Yisrael (v3). One medieval reading takes “in Yisrael” to leave out the mixed crowd that came out with them (Exod 12:38). The count is military throughout: men of fighting age, which is why women, those under twenty and the Levites stand outside it.
    3. tribe (v4). The word here, maṭṭeh, is literally “staff.” The tribe is named by its leader’s rod, which matters when twelve staffs are laid before the presence in chapter 17.
    4. Elishama (v10). By the genealogy of 1 Chr 7:26–27 Ephrayim’s chieftain is the grandfather of Yehoshua (Ammihud, Elishama, Non, Yehoshua). Of all the men counted in this chapter, only Kalev and Yehoshua will be left at the second count (26:64–65).
    5. called of the community (v16). The written text and the reading tradition differ by one vowel-letter here, without change of sense. One medieval reading takes the phrase to mean those who act for the community only when summoned, and each chieftain as presiding over all the thousands of his tribe.
    6. designated (v17). The verb, naqav, is “pierce” or “mark out”: the twelve were named one by one in verses 5–15.
    7. declared their descent (v18). A reflexive form of “bear, beget,” found only here: each man had his birth-line registered. One medieval reading notes that the line is reckoned through the father, not the mother, which is what “by their fathers’ house” says.
    8. the order of the count (vv20–44). The list in 1:5–15 follows the mothers’ groups; the count moves Gad up to third, beside Reuven and Shimon, the order in which the tribes will camp and march in chapter 2.
    9. six hundred thousand (v46). Taken at face value, 603,550 fighting men implies a people of perhaps two million in the wilderness. Some read ʾelef, “thousand,” as a clan or a military unit instead, which would make the totals far smaller; others hold to “thousand” and read the figures as they stand. The question is real and not settled here: the translation gives the numbers as the text does, and the identical total at Exod 38:26 has to be held in view by any reading. That the two totals match was already a question for medieval interpreters. Behind the count stands Gen 15:5: Abram was told to count the stars if he could, “so shall your seed be”; here the seed is counted. Chapter 26 returns to the same question.
    10. not counted (v49). The exemption rests on nothing but the command; one medieval reading adds that the Levites’ charge itself exempts them from the army, since the count is a count of soldiers.
    11. stranger (v51). The same word, zar, bars the outsider from the anointing oil (Exod 30:33) and from holy food (Lev 22:10). The cordon line returns at 3:10 and 3:38 and anchors chapter 18.
    12. no wrath on the community (v53). One medieval reading takes the ring as keeping any Israelite from drawing near and dying, as Uzza died beside the chest (2 Sam 6:7), and “wrath on the community” as wrath on any single member of it.
