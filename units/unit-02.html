The Book of Numbers · Study Translation

The Camp around the Tent

Unit 2 · Numbers 2:1–34

    The order of the camp; Yehudah to the east · 2:1–9

    1 And Yahweh spoke to Moshe and to Aharon, saying:

    2 “Each man by his company, with signs for their fathers’ house, Yisrael’s sons shall camp; facing, at a distance, around the Tent of Meeting they shall camp.123

    company — the tribal unit under one standard; signs — the visible emblems of each fathers’ house. The people keep their distance, facing inward.
    Gen 1:14 — the same word, signs: there the lights serve as signs for the set times; here signs orient a people in space.
    Exod 19:2 — at Sinai Yisrael camped in front of the mountain. The same shape, a camp facing a holy centre; there a mountain, here the Tent.

    The Tent pitched outside the camp Exod 33:3–11

    After the golden calf Yahweh said he would not go up in the midst of a stiff-necked people, or he would destroy them on the way. Moshe took the Tent and pitched it outside the camp, at some distance from it, and called it the Tent of Meeting. Anyone who sought Yahweh went out to it, and there Yahweh spoke with Moshe face to face.

    • The same Tent of Meeting (ʾohel moʿed) and the same camp (maḥaneh).
    • Distance between the people and the Tent in both.

    In Exodus the Tent stands at a distance, outside the camp. Here the people keep the distance, facing the Tent from all four sides, and the Tent stands in the midst of the camps (2:17). The distance has changed hands: once a withdrawal Yahweh imposed, now a clearance the people keep.

    The book’s first camp order reverses Exodus 33 and draws the promise of a Dwelling in their midst (Exod 25:8; Lev 26:11) as a picture. The picture is under strain: the cordon warns that no stranger may come near, and the rebellions to come set the people against this centre.

    3 Those camping toward the front, toward the sunrise: the company of the camp of Yehudah, by their armies; and the chieftain of the sons of Yehudah, Nachshon son of Amminadav.45

    toward the front, toward the sunrise — the east, named twice over; it is the side the Tent’s entrance faces.
    Exod 6:23; Ruth 4:20; Matt 1:4 — Aharon married Elisheva, Amminadav’s daughter and Nachshon’s sister, so the lead chieftain of the lead camp is Aharon’s brother-in-law. Ruth and Matthew place Amminadav and Nachshon on the line from Perets to David.

    Yaakov’s household and his blessings Gen 35:22–26; 48:17–20; 49:3–4, 8–10

    Yaakov had twelve sons by four mothers: Leah, Rachel, and the two handmaids, Bilhah and Zilpah. Blessing Yoseph’s sons, he crossed his hands and set Ephrayim, the younger, before Menasheh. On his deathbed he told Reuven, the firstborn, that he would no longer be first, and called Yehudah a lion’s whelp from whom the scepter would not depart.

    • The camps follow the mothers. East: Leah’s Yehudah, Yissakhar and Zevulun. South: Leah’s Reuven and Shimon, with Zilpah’s Gad. West: Rachel’s line, Ephrayim and Menasheh from Yoseph, with Binyamin. North: the handmaids’ sons, Dan and Naphtali from Bilhah and Asher from Zilpah.
    • Yehudah is first; Reuven keeps a camp, but in second place; Ephrayim leads the west, ahead of Menasheh.

    Levi, a son of Leah, stands outside the twelve camps. Yoseph is split in two. Gad, Zilpah’s son, sits among Leah’s sons. The chapter begins with Yehudah, where the first chapter began with Reuven.

    The camp may be the patriarch’s blessings drawn in space, with the Tent at the centre where a father’s bed would be. The pattern is plain to see; the text never says it is designed.

    4 And his army and their accounted: seventy-four thousand six hundred.

    5 And those camping beside him: the tribe of Yissakhar; and the chieftain of the sons of Yissakhar, Netanel son of Tsuar.

    6 And his army and its accounted: fifty-four thousand four hundred.

    7 The tribe of Zevulun; and the chieftain of the sons of Zevulun, Eliav son of Chelon.6

    8 And his army and its accounted: fifty-seven thousand four hundred.

    9 All the accounted of the camp of Yehudah: a hundred thousand and eighty thousand and six thousand and four hundred, by their armies. They shall set out first.

    The camp is also the marching order: each company sets out in turn, Yehudah’s first.
    Judg 1:1–2 — after Yehoshua’s death the people ask Yahweh who shall go up first against the Canaanites, and the answer is Yehudah. There a question put to Yahweh; here a fixed order.

    The Tent in the midst · 2:17

    17 And the Tent of Meeting shall set out, the camp of the Levites, in the midst of the camps. As they camp, so they shall set out, each man in his place, by their companies.9101112

    Set apart by paragraph breaks on both sides, the Tent’s sentence stands between the second and third camps: the centre of the march as of the camp.

    Ephrayim to the west · 2:18–24

    18 The company of the camp of Ephrayim, seaward, by their armies; and the chieftain of the sons of Ephrayim, Elishama son of Ammihud.

    seaward — toward the sea, that is, the west.
    1 Chr 7:26–27 — on Chronicles’ line of Ephrayim, Ammihud, Elishama, Nun and Yehoshua follow one another. Name, father and tribe agree, so the western chieftain would be the grandfather of the man who leads the next generation. Chronicles does not say it is the same Elishama.

    21 And his army and their accounted: thirty-two thousand two hundred.

    22 And the tribe of Binyamin; and the chieftain of the sons of Binyamin, Avidan son of Gidoni.

    23 And his army and their accounted: thirty-five thousand four hundred.

    24 All the accounted of the camp of Ephrayim: a hundred thousand and eight thousand and a hundred, by their armies. They shall set out third.

    The fourth side closes the square, and Yahweh’s speech ends.

    The square around the centre comes back as a city Ezek 48:30–35; Rev 21:12–13

    Ezekiel’s closing vision gives the restored city twelve gates named for the tribes, three on each side, and a new name: Yahweh Is There. Earlier in the book the glory had left the city eastward (Ezek 11:23) and later returned by the east gate (Ezek 43:2–4). Revelation’s new Jerusalem has twelve gates bearing the names of the twelve tribes, three on each of four sides.

    • Three tribes to each of four sides, each tribe named.
    • The holy centre in the middle.

    In Ezekiel Levi is inside the count and Yoseph is one tribe; Yehudah is no longer on the east, and the grouping by mother is gone. The centre is a name, Yahweh Is There, where Numbers has a Tent. The sides come in three orders: east, south, west, north here; north, east, south, west in Ezekiel; east, north, south, west in Revelation, which assigns no tribe to a side.

    Numbers 2 is the canon’s earliest picture of a people arranged around the place where Yahweh is. Ezekiel and Revelation reuse the shape for a presence restored after exile: the promise of Yahweh in their midst mapped here, withdrawn when the glory leaves, and drawn again. Creation, covenant, exile and presence meet in one image.

    The sum, the Levites and the doing · 2:32–34

    32 These are the accounted of Yisrael’s sons by their fathers’ house; all the accounted of the camps by their armies: six hundred thousand and three thousand and five hundred and fifty.15

    The same total as the first accounting (1:46): the counted men are reseated, not recounted.

    33 But the Levites were not accounted among Yisrael’s sons, just as Yahweh commanded Moshe.16

    34 And Yisrael’s sons did all that Yahweh commanded Moshe: so they camped by their companies, and so they set out, each man by his clans, by his fathers’ house.

    The same compliance formula closed chapter 1 (1:54): the two chapters are twin movements of one scene.
    Gen 6:22; Exod 39:32 — Noach did just as God commanded him, and the Tabernacle’s makers did just as Yahweh commanded Moshe. There the obedience makes something; here it is the people’s own camping and marching.

    Notes

    1. signs (v2). The text does not say what the signs looked like. Later Jewish tradition supplied them: a cloth for each tribe in the colour of its stone on the high priest’s breastplate; positions matching the places Yaakov’s sons took around his bier; and, for the four leading companies, the four faces of Ezekiel’s living creatures (lion, man, ox, eagle). Genesis names no positions for the bier and Numbers names no figures, so these are tradition, not the text.
    2. facing, at a distance (v2). The word (mineged) means literally “from opposite”. Some read a measured distance (about a mile, so the Tent could still be reached on the Sabbath); others read simply “a good way off”. The rendering keeps both senses audible.
    3. company (v2). Older versions say “standard” or “banner”. The word (degel) appears elsewhere only once, in the Song of Songs, where its meaning is uncertain. Garrison documents from Elephantine in Egypt and from Arad use it for a military unit, and this verse keeps a separate word, ʾotot, “signs”, for the visible emblems. Hence “company”: the unit, not the flag.
    4. toward the front, toward the sunrise (v3). The phrase qedmah mizraḥah is the one Exodus uses for the east side of the courtyard, where the entrance is (Exod 27:13; 38:13). It joins two ways of naming direction: by what lies in front, and by the sun.
    5. east and Eden (v3, tentative). Some readers connect the east with Eden: the cherubim guard the garden’s way from the east (Gen 3:24), and Qayin settles east of Eden (Gen 4:16). On that reading the entrance side, held by Yehudah, faces the way back toward the presence. Numbers 2 never mentions Eden, and the reading rests on a few commentators, so it is offered as a possibility.
    6. Eliav son of Chelon (v7). Not the Eliav of Reuven whose sons Datan and Aviram rebel in chapter 16. Same name, different man.
    7. southward (v10). The word (temanah) means literally “to the right”. The chapter’s directions are those of a person facing east, toward the sunrise, so the south is on the right and the sea, the west, is behind.
    8. Reuel (v14). The father of Gad’s chieftain is Reuel here and Deuel at 1:14. The translation gives each form as written. Why the forms differ is not settled.
    9. the Tent on the march (v17). Here the Tent moves as one unit between the second and third companies. Chapter 10 has the Dwelling taken down and carried by one group of Levite clans after Yehudah’s company, and the holy objects carried by another after Reuven’s. Whether chapter 10 simply carries out this order or refines it is left open here.
    10. in his place (v17). Literally “at his hand”: the area within a man’s reach, his own station.
    11. pitching a tent (v17, tentative). The Gospel of John says the Word became flesh and pitched his tent among us (John 1:14; the Greek verb skēnoō, “to tent, to dwell in a tent”). Read beside this verse, the Tent in the midst of the camps becomes a picture John may draw on. English versions say “dwelt”, which hides the tent, and the link runs as much through Exodus as through Numbers.
    12. outside the camp (v17, tentative). Hebrews urges its readers to go out to Jesus outside the camp (Heb 13:11–14; Greek parembolē, “camp”), where the sin-offering bodies were burned. That reverses this chapter’s picture, where the holy centre is inside. But Hebrews draws mainly on the Day of Atonement and on Exodus 33, not on this chapter.
    13. Dan last (v25, tentative). Yaakov’s blessing calls Dan a serpent by the road that bites the horse’s heels (Gen 49:16–17), an image suited to a rearguard. Numbers 2 says nothing of it; the link is a reader’s association.
    14. Dan in the north (v25, tentative). Later the tribe of Dan sets up its own image and priesthood (Judg 18:30–31), and the northern king places a golden calf at Dan (1 Kgs 12:28–30), so the edge of the camp became the edge of faithfulness. The north is shared with Asher and Naphtali, and the chapter gives no hint of this; at most it is hindsight.
    15. the totals (v32). The four camp totals are the chapter’s own figures, and they add up to the grand total of the first accounting. How such large numbers should be read is discussed at 1:46.
    16. among (v33). The word (tavekh, “midst”) is the same as in v17: the Levites camp in the midst of the camps, yet are not accounted in the midst of Yisrael’s sons. Chapter 3 explains the difference.
