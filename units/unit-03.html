The Book of Numbers · Study Translation

Aharon's line and the Levites in place of the firstborn

Unit 3 · Numbers 3:1–51

    Aharon's line · 3:1–4

    1 These are the generations of Aharon and Moshe, on the day Yahweh spoke with Moshe on Mount Sinai.1

    generations — Genesis's own heading formula. The list that follows names only Aharon's sons.

    2 These are the names of Aharon's sons: the firstborn Nadav, and Avihu, Elazar and Itamar.

    3 These are the names of Aharon's sons, the anointed priests, whose hand he filled to serve as priests.

    anointed … filled their hand — two installation terms. Here all the sons are anointed; elsewhere, Aharon alone.

    4 But Nadav and Avihu died before Yahweh when they brought near strange fire before Yahweh in the wilderness of Sinai, and they had no sons. So Elazar and Itamar served as priests in the lifetime of Aharon their father.2

    in the lifetime of — or "in the presence of"; the Hebrew allows both.
    Exod 24:1, 9–11 — Nadav and Avihu went up the mountain, saw the God of Yisrael, and ate and drank. Here they die before Yahweh.

    The tribe of Levi brought near · 3:5–10

    5 Yahweh spoke to Moshe:

    6 "Bring near the tribe of Levi and station it before Aharon the priest, and they will attend him.

    7 They will keep his charge and the charge of the whole community before the Tent of Meeting, to do the work of the Dwelling.3

    Gen 2:15 — the man set in the garden "to work it and to keep it." The same pair of verbs names the Levites' service here.

    8 They will keep all the equipment of the Tent of Meeting and the charge of the Israelites, to do the work of the Dwelling.

    9 You will give the Levites to Aharon and to his sons; given, given are they to him from among the Israelites.

    given, given — the doubling is emphatic: wholly handed over.
    reads "they are given to me," as at 8:16.

    10 And Aharon and his sons you will appoint, and they will keep their priesthood; and the stranger who draws near will be put to death."

    appoint — the accounting verb again, here in its sense of putting someone in charge.

    Mine are the firstborn · 3:11–13

    11 Yahweh spoke to Moshe:

    12 "And I — look, I have taken the Levites from among the Israelites in place of every firstborn, what opens the womb among the Israelites; the Levites will be mine.4

    adds "they will be their ransom," and renders "in place of" with anti.
    Mark 10:45 — the Son of Man gives his life "a ransom in place of many"; 1 Pet 1:18–19 — ransomed "not with silver or gold."

    13 For every firstborn is mine. On the day I struck down every firstborn in the land of Egypt I set apart for myself every firstborn in Yisrael, from human to beast. Mine they will be. I am Yahweh."

    Exod 13:2, 12–15 — every firstborn, what opens the womb, is Yahweh's because of the plague; Exod 12:12 ends that judgment with the same "I am Yahweh."

    Accounting for Levi · 3:14–39

    14 Yahweh spoke to Moshe in the wilderness of Sinai:

    15 "Take account of the sons of Levi by their ancestral houses, by their clans; every male from a month old and up you will take account of them."

    from a month old — not twenty: Levi is counted as a hereditary, devoted tribe, not as an army.

    16 So Moshe took account of them at the mouth of Yahweh, as he had been commanded.

    at the mouth of Yahweh — the command formula that later governs the cloud (9:18–23).

    17 These were the sons of Levi by their names: Gershon, Kehat and Merari.

    18 These are the names of the sons of Gershon by their clans: Livni and Shimi.

    19 The sons of Kehat by their clans: Amram and Yitshar, Chevron and Uzziel.5

    20 The sons of Merari by their clans: Machli and Mushi. These are the clans of the Levites by their ancestral houses.

    The clan names follow Exod 6:16–19. Vv. 21–37 repeat one formula per house (clans, count, camp side, chieftain, charge), set here as a table.

    The firstborn accounted for · 3:40–43

    40 Yahweh said to Moshe: "Take account of every firstborn male of the Israelites from a month old and up, and take up the number of their names.

    41 And take the Levites for me — I am Yahweh — in place of every firstborn among the Israelites, and the Levites' livestock in place of every firstborn among the Israelites' livestock."

    livestock — the animal firstborn are covered the same way, though v. 42 does not follow the clause up.

    42 So Moshe took account, as Yahweh had commanded him, of every firstborn among the Israelites.

    43 And every firstborn male by the number of names, from a month old and up, of those accounted for among them, was twenty-two thousand two hundred and seventy-three.

    The redemption price · 3:44–51

    44 Yahweh spoke to Moshe:

    45 "Take the Levites in place of every firstborn among the Israelites, and the Levites' livestock in place of their livestock; the Levites will be mine. I am Yahweh.

    46 As the redemption price of the two hundred and seventy-three of the Israelites' firstborn who are surplus over the Levites,

    47 you will take five shekels apiece per skull; by the shekel of the holy place you will take it, twenty gerahs to the shekel.7

    per skull — the census word of 1:2. Five shekels is the value set on a boy over a month old.

    48 And you will give the silver to Aharon and to his sons, the redemption price of the surplus among them."

    49 So Moshe took the silver of the redemption price from those surplus over those redeemed by the Levites.

    50 From the firstborn of the Israelites he took the silver: one thousand three hundred and sixty-five, by the shekel of the holy place.

    51 And Moshe gave the silver of the redemption price to Aharon and to his sons at the mouth of Yahweh, as Yahweh had commanded Moshe.

    as Yahweh had commanded — the compliance formula that closed 1:54 and 2:34 closes the ledger too.

    Notes

    1. the generations of Aharon and Moshe (v1). The heading promises both men's descendants, but only Aharon's sons follow, and Aharon is named first, as in Exod 6:26 before the order flips in 6:27; the date clause matches Exod 6:28. Moshe's sons, Gershom and Eliezer (Exod 2:22; 18:3–4), are "named among the tribe of Levi" (1 Chr 23:14), not among the priests; a grandson, Shevuel son of Gershom, keeps the treasuries (1 Chr 26:24). Judg 18:30 names another: Yehonatan son of Gershom, priest at Dan's idol shrine "until the day the land went into exile." There the Hebrew spells the grandfather Menasheh with a raised letter nun; without it the name reads Moshe, and the Latin Vulgate reads Moses. One rabbinic reading explains the heading differently: Moshe taught Aharon's sons, so they count as his. Others suspect a list of Moshe's descendants has dropped out.
    2. and they had no sons (v4). A family line opens by recording a line cut off in the wilderness, before any complaint has been voiced. The second accounting repeats the death notice (26:61), 1 Chr 24:2 repeats "they had no sons," and the phrase returns for Tselophchad (27:3), whose daughters turn it into a claim to inheritance. Elazar's succession comes at 20:25–28, and Deut 10:6 remembers it.
    3. keep … work (v7). Whether "keep the charge" here means standing guard or simply doing assigned duties is disputed. The guarding reading has an ancient parallel: a Hittite text of instructions for temple staff divides custody of the temple between priests and a lower rank on night watch, with death for intruders. It also strengthens the Eden link, since the last guards set at the east were the cherubim of Gen 3:24, and here the leaders camp on the east (v38). The same "work of the Dwelling" names its building (Exod 39:32) before it names its keeping.
    4. I have taken the Levites (v12). The text grounds the exchange in the plague on Egypt. Rabbinic tradition adds the golden calf: the firstborn lost the service, and the Levites, who rallied to Moshe, "filled their hand" for Yahweh (Exod 32:26–29). Levi's zeal began darker, at Shekhem (Gen 34) and under Yaakov's curse, "I will scatter them in Yisrael" (Gen 49:5–7). Deut 33:8–11 turns it to blessing, and Mal 2:4–7 calls it Yahweh's "covenant with Levi."
    5. Yitshar … Uzziel (v19). Korach is Yitshar's son (Exod 6:21; Num 16:1). The chieftain of Kehat is Elitsaphan son of Uzziel (v30), the cousin who carried out Nadav and Avihu's bodies (Lev 10:4). One midrashic reading makes this appointment, of the youngest brother's son, the grievance behind Korach's revolt. The camp map adds that Kehat camps south, beside Reuven's standard (2:10), the tribe of Datan and Aviram.
    6. twenty-two thousand (v39). The three houses total 22,300 (7,500 + 8,600 + 6,200). One explanation is a scribal slip: in v28, "six hundred" and "three hundred" differ by a single Hebrew letter. A rabbinic answer holds that the 300 were themselves firstborn and could not stand in for another firstborn. Rounding is ruled out, because the chapter counts exactly elsewhere (22,273; 273). On the size of the census numbers generally, see the first accounting (ch. 1).
    7. five shekels apiece (v47). Lev 27:6 values a boy from a month to five years at five shekels. The formula "twenty gerahs to the shekel" comes from the census ransom of Exod 30:12–13, paid "so that there be no plague." The redemption becomes permanent at 18:15–16. Luke 2:22–23 has Jesus presented under the firstborn law of Exod 13 but mentions only the purification offering, not the five shekels.
