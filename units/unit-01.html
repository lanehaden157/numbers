The Book of Numbers · Study Translation

The First Accounting

Unit 1 · Numbers 1:1–54

    Yahweh orders an accounting · 1:1–4

    1 Yahweh spoke to Moshe in the wilderness of Sinai, in the Tent of Meeting, on the first of the second month, in the second year after their going out from the land of Egypt:1

    second month — a month after the Dwelling was set up (Exod 40:17); chs. 7 and 9 later go back before this date.

    2 “Lift the head of the whole community of Yisrael’s sons — by their clans, by their fathers’ house, by the number of names, every male, by their skulls.2

    lift the head — the idiom for taking a sum; by their skulls, head by head.
    Isa 40:26; Ps 147:4 — God brings out the starry host “by number” and calls each “by name.” The promise that Avraham’s seed would be as the stars (Gen 15:5; 22:17) is here being counted out, name by name.

    3 From twenty years old and up, everyone going out to the army in Yisrael — y’all shall take account of them by their armies, you and Aharon.3

    going out to the army — fit for war; this is an accounting of fighting men, not a head-count of everyone.

    4 And with y’all there shall be a man for each tribe, each man head of his fathers’ house.

    The chieftains · 1:5–16

    5 These are the names of the men who will stand with y’all: for Reuven, Elitsur son of Shedeur;

    6 for Shimon, Shelumiel son of Tsurishaddai;

    7 for Yehudah, Nachshon son of Amminadav;

    Ruth 4:19–20; 1 Chr 2:10–11; Matt 1:4; Luke 3:32–33 — Yehudah’s wilderness chieftain and his father stand in David’s line, and in the Gospels’ line of Jesus.

    8 for Yissakhar, Netanel son of Tsuar;

    9 for Zevulun, Eliav son of Chelon;

    10 for the sons of Yosef: for Efrayim, Elishama son of Ammihud; for Menasheh, Gamliel son of Pedahtsur;4

    sons of Yosef — Yosef counts as two tribes, which keeps the number at twelve once Levi is set aside.

    11 for Binyamin, Avidan son of Gidoni;

    12 for Dan, Achiezer son of Ammishaddai;5

    13 for Asher, Pagiel son of Okhran;

    14 for Gad, Eliasaf son of Deuel;

    15 for Naftali, Achira son of Enan.”

    16 These were the summoned of the community, chieftains of their fathers’ tribes; they were heads of the thousands of Yisrael.6

    summoned of the community — a standing title for those called whenever the whole body acts.

    The accounting taken · 1:17–19

    17 And Moshe and Aharon took these men who had been designated by names,

    18 and they assembled the whole community on the first of the second month, and they declared their descent by their clans, by their fathers’ house, by the number of names, from twenty years old and up, by their skulls,7

    declared their descent — each man registered his lineage; the verb is “bear, beget,” turned reflexive.

    19 as Yahweh commanded Moshe. And he took account of them in the wilderness of Sinai.

    The count by tribe · 1:20–46

    20 The sons of Reuven, Yisrael’s firstborn — their generations, by their clans, by their fathers’ house, by the number of names, by their skulls, every male from twenty years old and up, everyone going out to the army —8

    21 their accounted-for, for the tribe of Reuven: forty-six thousand five hundred.9

    the pattern — verses 22–43 repeat this formula word for word, changing only tribe and total; they are set out as a table below, with Reuven’s count and the total.

    603,550 — the text’s own total; how to read “thousand” here is a real question (see note).
    Num 26:64–65 — the second accounting counts again and says outright that not one man of this count remains, except Kalev and Yehoshua.
    2 Sam 24:1–10; 1 Chr 21:1–8 — David’s census, taken on his own initiative, is judged as sin and brings plague; here the count is commanded and carried out without harm.

    Levi set apart · 1:47–54

    47 But the Levites, by their fathers’ tribe, were not accounted for among them.

    48 Yahweh spoke to Moshe:

    49 “Only the tribe of Levi you shall not take account of, and their head you shall not lift among Yisrael’s sons.11

    50 Rather, appoint the Levites over the Dwelling of the Testimony, over all its vessels and over all that belongs to it. They shall carry the Dwelling and all its vessels, and they shall serve it, and around the Dwelling they shall camp.12

    appoint — the same verb as “take account of”: Levi is not accounted for, but appointed.

    51 When the Dwelling sets out, the Levites shall take it down; when the Dwelling camps, the Levites shall set it up; and the outsider who comes near shall be put to death.13

    outsider — here anyone not a Levite, not a foreigner.

    52 Yisrael’s sons shall camp each man at his camp and each man by his banner, by their armies.

    banner — the tribal standards are laid out in the next chapter.

    53 But the Levites shall camp around the Dwelling of the Testimony, so there is no wrath on the community of Yisrael’s sons; and the Levites shall keep the charge of the Dwelling of the Testimony.”

    so there is no wrath — the Levite ring shields the camp from the holiness at its center.

    54 Yisrael’s sons did it; according to all that Yahweh commanded Moshe, so they did.14

    Notes

    1. wilderness of Sinai (v1). The phrase brackets the whole Sinai stay: Yisrael arrives in “the wilderness of Sinai” at Exod 19:1–2, the priestly laws are placed there at Lev 7:38, and the camp leaves it at Num 10:12. This census stands at the far end of that stay. The date also sits in tension with the chapters that follow: 7:1 and 9:1 describe events a month earlier, so chapters 1–10 are arranged by subject, not strict chronology.
    2. fathers’ house (v2). The count nests three kin-units: tribe, “fathers’ house” (a mid-level lineage), and clan. How the fathers’ house worked socially is debated; some read it as a later social structure described in wilderness terms. Tribal membership runs through the father, which settles cases of mixed parentage.
    3. twenty years old and up (v3). No upper age is given, unlike the Levite count in 4:3 (thirty to fifty). Lists of fighting men by clan and lineage are well attested as administrative records elsewhere in the ancient Near East, and the formula here likely preserves that kind of tabular source.
    4. Efrayim and Menasheh (v10). Yaakov adopts Yosef’s two sons: “Efrayim and Menasheh shall be mine, as Reuven and Shimon are” (Gen 48:5). The list carries out that blessing without citing it, and Levi’s removal (vv47–53) is what makes room for both sons.
    5. Dan (v12). The phrase “the tribe of Dan” recurs for Oholiav, a craftsman of the Dwelling (Exod 31:6; 38:23), and for the father of the blasphemer stoned in Lev 24:11. Dan’s later history is uneven: it migrates north and sets up its own shrine (Judg 18), and it is missing from the sealed tribes of Rev 7:5–8.
    6. thousands (v16). The word rendered “thousand” (ʾelef) can also name a clan or a military unit (Judg 6:15, “my ʾelef is the weakest in Menasheh”). Here the chieftains are “heads of the ʾalfe of Yisrael,” which bears on how the totals in vv20–46 are read (see note 10).
    7. declared their descent (v18). The verb is the reflexive of “bear, beget,” used this way only here: each man had himself entered by his birth-line. The grammar matters because it makes the census a genealogy as well as a head-count, which is why the formula keeps saying “their generations.”
    8. Reuven, Yisrael’s firstborn (v20). Reuven keeps the title and first place, but Yaakov’s blessing denies him preeminence (Gen 49:3–4), and 1 Chr 5:1 says his birthright went to “the sons of Yosef.” The double portion that places Efrayim and Menasheh in this list is that birthright. Yehudah, not Reuven, will lead the march (2:3; 10:14).
    9. the order of the tribes (vv20–43). The chieftain list (vv5–15) puts Gad eleventh, but the count moves Gad to third, after Reuven and Shimon. That is the order of the southern camp in 2:10–16, so the count already anticipates the camp arrangement.
    10. six hundred three thousand five hundred fifty (v46). The total, repeated in Exod 38:26 for the ransom levy, implies a camp of perhaps two million once women, children, the old and the Levites are included. That is hard to reconcile with a wilderness setting, and proposals vary. Some take the numbers at face value as a claim of miraculous provision. Others read ʾelef as a clan or squad, which yields a far smaller host, though that reading strains against the book’s own sums. Still others see the totals as symbolic or schematic. Every tribal total is rounded to hundreds (Gad’s to fifties), which suggests a stylized list. The text is translated as it stands; the question is left open.
    11. Levi not accounted for (v49). Two readings of the exemption stand side by side in the tradition. One sees it as honor: the king’s own guard is counted apart from the general levy. The other looks ahead to 14:29, where everyone “accounted for” from twenty up is sentenced to die in the wilderness; on this view Levi, which held back from the golden calf (Exod 32:26), is kept off the list that the sentence will follow. The second reading gives the exemption a weight the chapter does not state, and it can be tested when chapter 14 is reached.
    12. Dwelling of the Testimony (v50). The same title closes the accounting of the Dwelling’s construction (Exod 38:21), and “around the Dwelling” echoes its completion (Exod 40:33). The Levites guard what Exodus built. “Testimony” refers to the tablets in the chest at its center.
    13. shall be put to death (v51). Readers differ on whether the outsider is executed by the Levite guards or struck by God. The passive form leaves it open; 18:3–7 and the Korach story (16:35) lean toward death from Yahweh.
    14. so they did (v54). The compliance formula (“as Yahweh commanded Moshe”) frames the unit (v19, v54) and recurs throughout chs. 1–10. The book’s second movement is built on its failure: from ch. 11 on, the people no longer simply do as commanded.
