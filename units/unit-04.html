The Book of Numbers · Study Translation

Carrying the holy things

Unit 4 · Numbers 4:1–49

    Kehat: wrapping the most holy things · 4:1–16

    1 Yahweh spoke to Moshe and to Aharon, saying:

    Both are addressed; by 4:19 Aharon's house answers for the Kehatites' lives.

    2 Lift the head of the sons of Kehat from among the sons of Levi, by their clans, by their ancestral houses,

    Lift the head — the census idiom of 1:2, built on the verb that will mean "carry" through the chapter.

    3 from thirty years old and upward to fifty years old, everyone who comes into the army to do work in the Tent of Meeting.

    Thirty to fifty here; 8:24 starts Levite service at twenty-five, and the fighting count of 1:3 at twenty.
    1 Chr 23:3, 24–27 counts the Levites from thirty, then from twenty, because they "no longer need to carry the Dwelling"; 2 Chr 35:3 lifts the chest off Levite shoulders for good.

    4 This is the service of the sons of Kehat in the Tent of Meeting: the most holy things.

    the most holy things — the objects themselves, not the inner room; the bronze altar outside is among them (Exod 30:29).

    5 When the camp sets out, Aharon and his sons shall come in and take down the screening curtain and cover the chest of the testimony with it.

    Exod 26:33 hung this curtain to divide the holy place from the most holy, and Exod 40:3, 21 screened the chest with it; on the road the dividing curtain becomes the chest's first wrapping. Heb 10:19–20 speaks of entering the holy places "through the curtain."

    6 They shall put on it a covering of tachash skin and spread a cloth wholly of blue over it, on top, and set its poles.1

    Only the chest wears its blue outermost; everything else travels with the leather on the outside.

    7 Over the table of the Presence they shall spread a cloth of blue, and put on it the dishes, the ladles, the bowls, and the jugs for pouring; and the continual bread shall be on it.

    table of the Presence — literally "table of the face." The bread stays on the table even on the march.

    8 They shall spread over them a cloth of scarlet and cover it with a covering of tachash skin, and set its poles.

    9 They shall take a cloth of blue and cover the lampstand of the light, and its lamps, and its tongs, and its firepans, and all its oil vessels with which they tend it.2

    10 They shall put it and all its vessels into a covering of tachash skin and put it on the carrying frame.

    11 Over the golden altar they shall spread a cloth of blue and cover it with a covering of tachash skin, and set its poles.

    12 They shall take all the vessels of ministry with which they minister in the holy place, and put them into a cloth of blue, and cover them with a covering of tachash skin, and put them on the carrying frame.

    13 They shall clear the altar of its fat-ash and spread a cloth of purple over it.

    clear of its fat-ash — one verb, from the word for the greasy ash of burnt offerings.

    14 They shall put on it all its vessels with which they minister on it: the firepans, the forks, the shovels, and the basins, all the vessels of the altar; and they shall spread over it a covering of tachash skin and set its poles.

    Jer 52:18–19 (and 2 Kgs 25:14–15) lists these same shovels, basins, firepans and bowls carried off to Babylon. Isa 52:11 calls the returning exiles "bearers of the vessels of Yahweh" who must touch nothing unclean, and Ezra 1:7–11; 8:28 counts the vessels out again: "you are holy to Yahweh, and the vessels are holy."

    15 When Aharon and his sons have finished covering the holy things and all the vessels of the holy place, when the camp sets out, after that the sons of Kehat shall come to carry; but they shall not touch the holy, and die. These are the burden of the sons of Kehat in the Tent of Meeting.3

    and die — the Hebrew states the consequence flatly, as if it were already part of the act.
    2 Sam 6:6–7: Uzzah takes hold of the chest on the road and dies; 1 Chr 15:2, 13–15 draws the lesson that only Levites carry it, on their shoulders with the poles, "as Moshe commanded."

    16 And the oversight of Elazar son of Aharon the priest: the oil for the light, the incense of spices, the continual grain offering, and the anointing oil — oversight of all the Dwelling and all that is in it, in the holy place and in its vessels.

    Lev 16:12–13: the high priest carries a firepan and incense of spices inside the curtain so that the cloud covers the chest's lid "and he does not die." The same curtain, firepan, spice incense, covering and death-warning are gathered here in 4:5–16.

    That they may live · 4:17–20

    17 Yahweh spoke to Moshe and to Aharon, saying:

    18 Do not cut off the tribe of the clans of the Kehatite from among the Levites.4

    cut off — the verb used for "cutting" a covenant; the danger to Kehat is laid on Moshe and Aharon.

    19 Do this for them, so that they live and do not die when they approach the most holy things: Aharon and his sons shall come in and set them, each man, to his service and to his burden.

    Within the book the danger comes true: firepans of incense bring fire on Korach's 250 (16:35), Elazar hammers those firepans into altar plating (16:37–39), the people cry "everyone who comes near … dies" (17:13), and 18:3 repeats "lest they die, they and you."

    20 But they shall not come in to see the holy, even for the space of a swallow, and die.5

    the space of a swallow — the time it takes to swallow: an instant.
    Exod 19:12, 21 set Sinai's two limits: whoever touches the mountain dies, and the people must not break through "to see" and perish. 4:15 takes the touch, 4:20 the sight; Exod 33:20 and 1 Sam 6:19 carry the sight-danger on.

    Gershon: the fabrics · 4:21–28

    21 Yahweh spoke to Moshe, saying:

    22 Lift the head of the sons of Gershon too, by their ancestral houses, by their clans.

    23 From thirty years old and upward to fifty years old you shall account for them, everyone who comes to enlist in the army, to serve the service in the Tent of Meeting.

    enlist in the army — verb and noun from one root; the verb is the one used for the women who "served" at the tent's entrance (Exod 38:8).

    24 This is the service of the clans of the Gershonite: to serve and to carry.

    25 They shall carry the curtains of the Dwelling and the Tent of Meeting, its covering and the covering of tachash that is on top of it, and the screen of the entrance of the Tent of Meeting,

    26 and the hangings of the court and the screen of the entrance of the gate of the court that is around the Dwelling and the altar, and their cords and all the vessels of their service, and all that is to be done with them; so they shall serve.

    Jer 10:20 laments "my tent is destroyed, and all my cords are snapped"; Isa 54:2 answers "lengthen your cords, strengthen your pegs," and Isa 33:20 promises a tent whose pegs are never pulled up again.

    27 At the mouth of Aharon and his sons shall be all the service of the sons of the Gershonite, for all their burden and all their service; and you shall appoint to them, as their charge, all their burden.

    appoint … as their charge — the counting verb and the guard-duty noun meet in one clause.

    28 This is the service of the clans of the sons of the Gershonite in the Tent of Meeting, and their charge is under the hand of Itamar son of Aharon the priest.

    Merari: the frame · 4:29–33

    29 The sons of Merari: by their clans, by their ancestral houses, you shall account for them.

    30 From thirty years old and upward to fifty years old you shall account for them, everyone who comes into the army to serve the service of the Tent of Meeting.

    31 And this is the charge of their burden, for all their service in the Tent of Meeting: the frames of the Dwelling and its bars and its posts and its bases,

    32 and the posts of the court all around and their bases and their pegs and their cords, for all their vessels and for all their service; and by names you shall account for the vessels of the charge of their burden.

    by names — the phrase that counted men in 1:2 now counts equipment.
    Exod 38:21 already applied this counting verb to the Dwelling's materials, "the work of the Levites, under the hand of Itamar son of Aharon the priest," the phrase that closes 4:28 and 4:33.

    33 This is the service of the clans of the sons of Merari, for all their service in the Tent of Meeting, under the hand of Itamar son of Aharon the priest.

    No scroll break follows: the orders run straight into the count. Vv. 34–45 repeat one report per house, set below as a table.

    Notes

    1. covering of tachash skin … set its poles (v6). The pack list is the build list. Every item the priests wrap and the Levites carry is named in the construction chapters of Exodus (25–27, 35–39), and the tachash covering that goes on top of each bundle is the same layer that roofed the Dwelling at rest (Exod 26:14). What taḥash was is disputed: some identify it with a sea animal's hide, others with a treated or coloured leather named by a borrowed trade term. On the poles, Exod 25:15 says they stay in the chest's rings and are never removed; "set its poles" may mean fitting or adjusting them for carrying rather than inserting them, and the tension is left open.
    2. the lampstand of the light (v9). The word for "light" here, maʾor, names in the Torah only the sky's lights of Gen 1:14–16 and the sanctuary lamp with its oil (Exod 25:6; 27:20; Lev 24:2). Gen 1:14 also gives the lights as "signs" and "appointed times," the words behind 2:2's signs and the Tent of Meeting's name. Each word is common on its own, so the cluster is suggestive rather than a deliberate allusion.
    3. the sons of Kehat shall come to carry (v15). Other texts give the carrying of the chest to different hands: the priests (Josh 3:6; 1 Kgs 8:3), the "Levitical priests" (Deut 31:9; Josh 3:3), the tribe of Levi (Deut 10:8), and the Levites (2 Chr 5:4). Numbers 4 divides the job: priests alone touch and wrap, and Levites lift what is already covered. Whether this division reflects an older practice or a later arrangement is argued both ways.
    4. the Kehatite (v18). The house this law shields is the house that produces Korach (16:1), yet "the sons of Korach did not die" (26:11). Later texts place Kehatites over the table bread every Sabbath (1 Chr 9:32), the table they once carried (4:7), and set Kehatites and Korachites leading Yisrael's praise (2 Chr 20:19).
    5. even for the space of a swallow (v20). The phrase kevalaʿ is literally "as a swallowing." Read as time, it means "for an instant," and Job 7:19's "until I swallow my spit" supports that sense. Another reading takes the verb as "wrap up, cover over": the Kehatites may not come in to watch the holy things while they are being wrapped. Both readings make sight, not only touch, the danger.
